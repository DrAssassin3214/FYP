### P1-01 - Ouga, Alinaitwe & Mwesige (2020) - block-laying productivity regression, Kampala, Uganda - USABLE AS EQUATION (comparable-context, not India)
- Authors: Allan Dickens Ouga, Henry Mwanaki Alinaitwe, Godfrey Mwesige (Makerere University).
- Title: Modelling Block Laying Productivity on Building Sites in Kampala.
- Journal: Journal of Construction in Developing Countries 25(1): 109-128, 2020. DOI 10.21315/jcdc2020.25.1.6 (Crossref-verified, title/authors/pages match).
- Access: full text read (PDF, 20 pp.). OPEN ACCESS: licence seen on PDF p.1 ("licensed under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) ... 4.0"); Crossref also lists CC BY 4.0.
- Country/context: Uganda (Kampala), 40 building sites; not India. Tag: international/developing-country (labour-intensive masonry, similar in spirit to India).
- Activity: solid concrete block laying (block 400 x 225 x 200 mm, length x thickness x height; 11 blocks/m2 coverage), work height 0.5-5.5 m (mid-value of height band), individual block layer (mason), porters (helpers) assigned.
- Data: on-site measurement sheets (area of wall built, start/end/rest times, porters, wall-height band) + questionnaire to block layers (experience, education). Sample size is stated inconsistently: abstract "40 building sites"; p.113 says productivities of "at least 160 block layers" were studied per Green's rule of thumb; p.114 says mean productivities of "83 block layers" for the 400x225x200 block; Table 2 (p.116) and the regression use n = 70.
- Model type: ordinary least squares multiple linear regression; four candidate models (Table 3, p.120); "best" = Model 3 by adjusted R2 and AIC.
- EXACT equation as printed (Eq. 3, p.119): `PRD = 0.53 - 0.08H + 0.40P + 0.36 EXED   (R2 = 0.65 and AIC = 51.16)`
- Variables (Table 2, p.116; definitions p.119):
  - PRD = block-laying productivity, m2 of wall built per hour per block layer (min 0.44, max 2.42, mean 1.28, SD 0.57).
  - H = work height, m (min 0.5, max 5.5, mean 2.60). Abstract says results limited to work heights not exceeding 6 m above ground.
  - P = number of porters assigned to a block layer (min 1, max 4, mean 2.0; 44 of 70 observations had exactly 2, p.116/p.123).
  - EXED = EX x ED: 1 if the block layer has >= 5 years experience AND a craft/diploma qualification, else 0 (EX: <5 y = 0, >5 y = 1; ED: none = 0, craft or diploma = 1).
- Coefficients (Table 3, Model 3, p.120): intercept 0.53 (t 3.85, p 0.000); H -0.08 (t -3.73); P 0.40 (t 6.25); EXED 0.36 (t 3.73). Adjusted R2 65%; AIC 51.16.
- Other models in Table 3 (read): Model 1 (all six terms) intercept 0.44, H -0.08, P 0.46, P2 -0.01, EX 0.02, ED 0.06, EXED 0.30, adj R2 64%, AIC 56.80. Model 2 intercept 0.49, H -0.08, P 0.40, EXED 0.30 (n.s.), adj R2 64%. Model 4 adds P2 to Model 3 (no gain).
- Output unit: m2 wall per block-layer-hour. To convert to a per-day figure the modeller must multiply by productive hours per day; the paper says the usual Uganda working day is 8 h (p.110) but does NOT give a per-day model.
- Fit: R2 0.65 (adjusted 65%); MAPE NR; RMSE NR; AIC 51.16. No hold-out validation seen in the text.
- Validity range (stated): Kampala building sites; work height up to 6 m; block 400 x 225 x 200 mm (abstract, p.109). Predictor ranges from Table 2.
- Table 1 (p.115) descriptive productivities (m2/h/block layer): means 0.70 to 1.37 across plinth / ground / first-floor bands and 2 block sizes (A = 400x225x200, B = 400x150x200).
- Limitations: (1) individual mason not crew; (2) P has almost no internal variability; (3) EX and ED individually n.s., only their interaction enters; (4) internal inconsistency on which block size the model uses (p.113-114 says 400x150x200 used in model development; p.119 says Eq. 3 is for 400x225x200); (5) inconsistent n (70/83/160); (6) concrete blocks in Uganda, not fired-clay brick in India; (7) no weather, mortar, bond pattern, or crew-size variable; (8) R2 0.65 means 35% unexplained.
- Evidence depth: FULL TEXT. Location: Eq. 3 p.119; Table 2 p.116; Table 3 p.120.
- Relevance to this FYP: only published Africa/Asia block-laying OLS equation with all coefficients printed; India transfer needs calibration. A mason-hour-to-day conversion and an H/P mapping are needed before use.

### P1-02 - Parthasarathy, Murugasan & Vasan (2018) - blockwork quantity regression from resource inputs, 52 tall residential projects, India - USABLE WITH STRONG CAUTION
- Authors: M. K. Parthasarathy, R. Murugasan, Ramya Vasan (L&T Construction; Anna University, Chennai).
- Title: Modelling manpower and equipment productivity in tall residential building projects in developing countries.
- Journal: Journal of the South African Institution of Civil Engineering 60(2): 23-33 (Paper 1547), June 2018. DOI 10.17159/2309-8775/2018/v60n2a3 (Crossref-verified).
- Access: free full text from SciELO South Africa (PDF read). No Creative Commons licence text found in the PDF; NOT marked open access (free-to-read only).
- Country/context: India, 52 tall (>= 30 m) framed RCC residential projects, data collected monthly for 18 months; five activities (concrete, reinforcement, formwork, blockwork, plastering). Tag: India-specific (but tall buildings with tower crane + hoist).
- Activity: blockwork (block type/size NR; unit m2). Manpower + equipment hours are inputs; monthly project-level quantity is the output.
- Method: multiple linear regression on sample data (70% of months) with every third month held out (30%) for validation (p.27); separate models for monsoon and non-monsoon months (Southwest monsoon end-May to early-Sept, Northeast monsoon Oct-Dec; four regional monsoon definitions A-D, p.27).
- EXACT equations as printed (p.29):
  - Model 10 (all months): `BW = 49.662 - 0.514 H + 0.183 MD + 2.887 TCH + 1.221 HH   (R2 = 0.932; N = 480; SE = 374.08)`
  - Model 11 (monsoon): `BW monsoon = 15.621 - 1.263 H + 0.176 MD + 2.529 TCH + 1.404 HH   (R2 = 0.936; N = 103; SE = 370.76)`
  - Model 12 (non-monsoon): `BW non-monsoon = -61.466 + 0.572 H + 0.234 MD + 2.753 TCH + 1.213 HH   (R2 = 0.933; N = 376; SE = 385.63)`
- Variables and units (Table 1, p.25; text p.28-29): BW = blockwork quantity, m2 per month per project (min 34, max 5,141, mean 2,605); H = height of building at which the work was done in the month, m (mean of start and end height; overall Table 1 range for H is 3-174 m, listed under the concrete block); MD = man-days per month (min 315, max 4,880, mean 1,538; labelled "MH" in Table 1); TCH = tower-crane hours allocated to blockwork (10% share of crane time) per month (min 86, max 1,088, mean 402); HH = hoist hours per month (min 141, max 2,506, mean 951).
- Table 3 (p.29) for Model 10: H -0.514 (t 1.691, p 0.045); MD 0.183 (t 6.792); TCH 2.887 (t 21.123); HH 1.221 (t 18.805); VIF 14.885. Table 4 (p.30): R2 0.932, adj R2 0.932, SE 374.088, n = 480.
- Output unit: m2 per month per project. NOT a per-mason or per-crew productivity.
- Fit: R2 0.932 (Model 10), MAPE NR, RMSE NR (SE 374.08 m2 quoted). Validation only by scatter plot vs out-of-sample data (Figure 4); no numerical validation metric seen.
- Limitations and cautions I found: (1) dependent variable is monthly quantity, so MD coefficient (0.183 m2 per man-day) is a partial regression coefficient confounded with crane and hoist hours (VIF ~14.9, multicollinearity acknowledged p.30); it must NOT be read as a mason's m2/day. (2) Tall buildings >= 30 m only, tower crane and hoist assumed; (3) intercept and small MD coefficient mean predictions are dominated by TCH and HH; (4) block type/wall thickness NR; (5) the paper allocates crane hours to activities by project-personnel judgement (10% for blockwork, p.25); (6) Table 1 label "MH" vs "MD" inconsistency.
- Evidence depth: FULL TEXT. Location: Models 10-12 p.29; Table 1 p.25; Tables 3-4 pp.29-30.
- Relevance to this FYP: shows an Indian monsoon vs non-monsoon split for blockwork (coefficients differ), but usable only if the app has monthly man-days, crane hours and hoist hours; not a crew m2/day predictor. Treat as context, not as the baseline productivity model.

### P1-05 - Thomas & Sudhakumar (2014) - masonry labour productivity multiple regression, Kerala, India - USABLE AS EQUATION (India-specific; laterite blocks)
- Authors: Anu V. Thomas, J. Sudhakumar (NIT Calicut, Kerala).
- Title: Modelling masonry labour productivity using multiple regression.
- Venue: In Raiden & Aboagye-Nimo (eds), Proceedings of the 30th Annual ARCOM Conference, 1-3 Sept 2014, Portsmouth, UK, pp. 1345-1354. No DOI (conference paper); OpenAlex lists the ARCOM PDF (https://www.arcom.ac.uk/-docs/proceedings/ar2014-1345-1354_Thomas_Sudhakumar.pdf), which I downloaded and read (10 pp.).
- Access: free full text on the ARCOM site; no licence statement seen in the PDF, so NOT marked open access.
- Country/context: India (Kerala), two high-rise RCC-framed projects (one multi-storey residential, one IT park) with masonry infill, built in the same period, 8 h day, 6 days/week.
- Activity: LATERITE block masonry in stretcher bond (not fired-clay brick; block size NR). Crews on subcontract (paid per masonry units placed; higher pay above 2.2 m) or directly employed labour.
- Data: daily-visit method; 152 observations, each = one crew-day (p.1347). Output measured next morning; site diary marked locations; time losses estimated by site staff; IMD hourly weather.
- Observed productivity: max 1.09, min 0.12, mean 0.41 m2/man-hr (p.1347).
- Model type: stepwise multiple linear regression in SPSS on a log10-transformed dependent variable (p.1348). Eleven candidate inputs (Table 1, p.1348): ratio of skilled to total labour, total labour, mode of employment, overtime, rainfall, temperature, relative humidity, delay-materials, time lost-rework, time lost-work not ready, work content. Only four terms were significant.
- Coefficients (Table 2, p.1350, unstandardised B): Constant -0.348 (SE 0.031); Mode of employment of labour 0.157 (SE 0.027, t 5.930); Overtime -0.024 (SE 0.006, t -4.124, beta -0.231); WC3_WC1 -0.220 (SE 0.025, t -8.652); WC2_WC1 -0.178 (SE 0.025, t -7.050); Delay - materials -0.024 (SE 0.009, t -2.685, beta -0.111).
- Equation: the paper prints Table 2 but NOT an explicit equation. The form below is RECONSTRUCTED by me from Table 2 plus the text statement that a log10 transform of the dependent variable was used (p.1348); read the dependent variable as log10(productivity in m2/man-hr): `log10(P) = -0.348 + 0.157*ModeEmp - 0.024*OT - 0.220*WC3 - 0.178*WC2 - 0.024*DelayMat`, so P = 10^(...). The paper does not print the back-transformation or a smearing correction; treat as an inference.
- Variable definitions: ModeEmp = 0 directly employed labour (reference), 1 subcontract labour; OT = overtime hours per day; DelayMat = hours per day lost to material unavailability (range 30 min to 5 h on the 57 of 152 observations that had a delay, otherwise 0; p.1347); WC2 = 1 for walls with numerous corners of height < 2.2 m; WC3 = 1 for walls of height > 2.2 m; WC1 (straight walls < 2.2 m) is the reference (p.1348). Overtime for subcontract crews rarely exceeded 1 h; directly employed labour averaged 3.7 h with a maximum of 8-9 h in two observations (p.1349).
- Output unit: m2 per man-hour (crew-day observation; per-man-hour). Convert to crew-day output = P x crew man-hours.
- Fit: R2 0.794, adjusted R2 0.787, F = 112.312 (p < 0.001) (pp.1348-1349). Split-sample validation 70/30: R2 0.783 (adj 0.772); MSE of model-building set 0.0117; MSPR on validation set 0.0119; MSE of full-data model 0.0113 (p.1352) - these are in log10 units (my inference; MSE of log10 productivity). Durbin-Watson 1.855 (p.1350). MAPE NR; RMSE NR (DERIVED from MSE 0.0113: about 0.106 in log10 units, my arithmetic, not printed).
- Non-significant: rainfall, temperature, relative humidity (paper says no weather relationship found in Kerala), rework loss, scaffolding-not-ready loss, crew size, skilled ratio.
- Limitations (paper p.1353 plus mine): two sites only; laterite blocks, not brick; work content scale limited to 3 levels; masonry called "interior job", weather not significant (may not hold for external walls in monsoon); overtime effect mostly from one site's directly employed labour; dependent variable in log10 so predictions are geometric-mean type; no mortar/bond/wall-thickness variable; validation is split-sample within the same two sites.
- Evidence depth: FULL TEXT. Location: Table 1 p.1348; Table 2 p.1350; validation p.1351-1352; observed productivity p.1347.
- Relevance to FYP: the only India-based (Kerala) explicit regression with masonry-relevant management variables (overtime, material delay, employment mode, wall complexity) printed in full. Good candidate for baseline plus disruption multipliers (material delay and overtime enter as exponents: 10^(-0.024 x hours)).

- Journal: ASTONJADRO: Jurnal Rekayasa Sipil 10(2): 260-270, 2021. DOI 10.32832/astonjadro.v10i2.4998 (Crossref-verified). PDF read (11 pp.). Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 is listed in Crossref metadata; licence text not seen in the PDF text itself.

## 4. Gaps (honest assessment for the FYP)

1. **No India-specific brick (fired-clay) masonry regression with a printed, verified equation and R2/MAPE was found and fully read.** The nearest India equation (P1-05, Thomas & Sudhakumar 2014) uses laterite blocks in Kerala, only 2 sites, 152 crew-days, and its "equation" is my own reconstruction from Table 2 (log10 back-transformation is not printed in the paper) — treat this as inference, not a directly quoted formula.
2. **Loganathan & Kalidindi (2015/2017), "Masonry labor construction productivity variation: an Indian case study"** (Tamil Nadu, 23 crews, 115 mm brick wall per some secondary snippets) could not be obtained as full text — ResearchGate required login and a CORE mirror returned HTTP 522 repeatedly. This is very likely the single most relevant missing paper; a supervisor/library subscription retrieval is recommended.
3. **No ANN/ML masonry productivity paper in the entire search publishes its trained weights**, so none of Gerek 2015, Lawaju 2021, Bokor 2021, Florez-Perez 2022, AlJassmi 2023, or the Iraqi ANN/SVM leads (Aswed 2016; Al-Zwainy SVM 2016) can be re-implemented from the paper alone; at best they give an input checklist and an accuracy/MAPE number.
4. **No factor-model / productivity-index x multiplier study for masonry** (in the Thomas & Yiakoumis / Hanna "baseline x factor" tradition) was found with a printed base rate and printed multiplier table specific to brick/block masonry; Sweis et al. (2009) only gives control limits, not multipliers, and only proposes (does not fit) a factor regression.
5. **No simulation model of bricklaying** (discrete-event or agent-based) with a published productivity distribution was found; the Florez crew-allocation line of work (2017 IGLC, 2017 MICE) uses illustrative, not fitted, numbers.
6. **Output units are inconsistent across the whole literature** (m2/hour, m2/day, m3/man-day, work-hours/m2, blocks/day) and wall thickness/brick size is very often unreported (Rasool & Al-Zwainy 2016; Paikun 2021 partially), which will require the app to state clearly which conversion assumptions (brick size, coursing, wall thickness) were used when adapting any of these coefficients.
7. **CPWD / Indian Standard Schedule of Rates (SSR) mason-output norms** (referenced qualitatively in Karthik & Rao's 2016 abstract as a comparator, e.g. IS 7272 Part I) were not retrieved as primary documents in this search; they are a distinct, non-academic source that should be fetched separately (government PDF, not a journal paper) if the tool needs an authoritative India baseline productivity figure.
8. Two country-appropriate ANN papers (Lawaju 2021 Nepal, Rana 2023 Nepal) and the Sri Lanka/Bangladesh/Pakistan lane produced only RII/ranking studies, not a single printed equation for masonry in those countries.
