## 3 Study design
- Type: cross-sectional questionnaire survey (RII ranking, ANOVA, exploratory factor analysis) plus 5 case-site visits (page 7-9).
- Country: India.
- Sample frame: 62 organizations chosen from 113 CIDC members (p.3). 151 completed questionnaires from 46 organizations (p.3).
- Respondents: 81 engineers and 70 supervisors (p.3). "More than one-third" had more than 10 years' experience (p.3).
- Attributes: 38 site amenity / labor welfare attributes, from literature and a pilot study (p.3).
- Scale: 5-point Likert, 1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree (p.3). Questionnaire in English and Hindi for supervisors; Google Forms and print (p.3). Question asks agreement that each item "decreases labour productivity" (Fig. 1, p.3).
- RII = [5n5+4n4+3n3+2n2+1n1] / [5(n1+...+n5)] (Eq. 1, p.3).
- ANOVA one-way at alpha = 0.01 between engineers and supervisors (p.3).
- Tests (p.3): standard error of skewness 0.197; kurtosis 0.392; Cronbach alpha 0.953; KMO 0.907; Bartlett significance 0.000. PCA with varimax rotation; factors by eigenvalue >1 and scree plot; loading cut-off 0.45 (p.5).
- Site visits (p.7): 5 projects (commercial office building, metro station, residential complex, company headquarters, high-rise building); contract value USD 60 to 135 million; about 3 months; productivity for formwork and reinforcement only.

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Gives an Indian, RII-ranked list of 38 labour-welfare and site-amenity attributes that respondents agreed lower labour productivity. These can serve as candidate risk factors (labour/welfare category) in a masonry risk register. It is not masonry-specific.
- Highest-ranked items (hazardous work without safety arrangements, safe drinking water, lighting, accommodation, wages, PPE) are plausible for masonry crews, but the paper does not test masonry.
- Method (5-point Likert agreement, RII Eq. 1, engineer vs supervisor split, ANOVA) is a usable template for the project's survey design.
- Case-site productivity (formwork, reinforcement) shows association between site-amenity score and productivity in 5 sites; no masonry productivity data. Association only, not shown as causal.
- Does not cover typical masonry-specific delay causes (material supply of bricks/mortar, scaffolding, weather, rework).

## 6 Limitations
- Sample of 151 from CIDC member organizations (large firms such as those named on p.3); small-contractor and unorganized-sector masonry crews may be under-represented. Own observation, from the sample description.
- Respondents were engineers and supervisors, not workers themselves.
- Perceptual agreement ("decreases productivity") rather than measured effect; RII measures perceived importance.
- Case study: only 5 sites, productivity only for formwork and reinforcement, one productivity figure per site; authors excluded loss due to materials, tools, drawings, weather; no statistical test relating score to productivity. Site scores are unweighted percentages of criteria met.
- Factor 8 has a single item; Site services item 0.443 is below the stated 0.45 cut-off (printed values).
- Authors state a direct cost-benefit link is "arguable" (p.9) and that a robust scale for site-amenity condition is still to be developed (p.9-10).
