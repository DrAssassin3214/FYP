## 3 Study design
- Type: literature-derived risk-factor list, three-round Delphi, then a Likert importance survey with RII and rank-exponent weights, producing a weighted index (CFRI). Owner's perspective. Not a probability x impact study; one importance scale only.
- Country/setting: UAE-based authors. The paper says only "construction industry" and mentions UAE only through a cited study (El-Sayegh 2008). The survey country is not stated explicitly (NR). Project type: general construction projects, no trade-level (masonry) focus.
- Stage 1 (Delphi): 44 factors from literature (14 client, 7 consultant, 15 contractor, 8 external; p.10-11). Panel of 8 experts (Table 7, p.10), all "+10" years experience: Contracts Manager (Developer); Manager x3 (Government Entity/Government Developer); Director (Real-estate Developer); Projects Manager (Contractor); Projects Manager (Consultant); Construction Manager (Contractor). Rule: retain factors selected by over 50% of experts (Table 5, p.8; text p.10 "50% or more").
  - Round 1: 44 -> 37 (7 irrelevant). Round 2: 37 -> 36 (1 irrelevant). Round 3: plateaued at the same 36. Final 36 (p.10).
  - After Delphi: 13 client, 6 consultant, 14 contractor, 3 external (p.11).
- Stage 2 (survey): 36 factors; sent to 40 respondents, 32 responses, return rate 80% (p.11). Five-point scale: 5 Extremely Important, 4 Very Important, 3 Important, 2 Somewhat Important, 1 Not Important (p.8).
- Analysis: RII = sum W / (A x N), sum W = 5n5+4n4+3n3+2n2+1n1, A = 5, N = 32 (Eq. 1-2, p.8). Weights by rank exponent: Wj = (n - rj + 1)^p / sum (n - rj + 1)^p, p = 2 (Eq. 3, p.8-9; the paper defines n as "number of respondents", although the table's weights fit n = 36 factors - see section 6). CFRI = 1 - sum(Wj x E), where E is user evaluation on the Table 6 scale: Very Low 0, Low 0.1, Medium Low 0.3, Medium 0.5, Medium-High 0.7, High 0.9, Very High 1 (p.9).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Method precedent only: shows a literature-list -> expert screening (Delphi, >50% retention) -> Likert survey -> RII ranking pipeline, with the RII formula printed (Eq. 1-2, N = 32, A = 5). This matches the project's RII approach.
- Several ranked factors overlap with generic masonry-delay causes at a general level: improper resource planning (rank 11), procurement delay (12), substandard work quality (8), failing to manage subcontractors (21), delay in progress (7), faulty construction methodology (2), unforeseen site conditions (29), changes in laws and regulations (28). These are general-construction wording, not masonry-specific.
- Weights Wj come from rank position only (rank exponent), not from RII magnitude, so they should not be quoted as measured importance. RII values themselves span only 0.63 to 0.86.
- Not usable as evidence about Indian conditions or about labour productivity of brick masonry: the factors are owner-side cash-flow risks (insolvency, payment delay, retention), the setting is not stated as India, and no trade-level data exist.
- The Delphi removal of safety, labour disputes and inflation reflects the panel's cash-flow framing (contractor-borne), so their absence says nothing about their importance for masonry delay.

## 6 Limitations
- Only RII-type importance; no probability x impact scoring; the CFRI uses user-entered linguistic evaluations, and the paper reports no application or validation on a real project (the abstract/conclusion say "validation of the index" but no validation result is presented in the text read).
- Delphi panel only 8 people, all 10+ years, mostly client/government side (2 contractor, 1 consultant of 8 as listed in Table 7).
- Survey 32 respondents; country/region of respondents not stated; no reliability (e.g. Cronbach alpha), no ranking-agreement test, and no tie-handling explanation (several RII ties, e.g. 0.83 for ranks 3-4, 0.64 for ranks 33-35).
- Table 8/9 contain duplicated factor names (Legal conflicts at ranks 19 and 24; Delays in response at 25 and 35), unexplained; the second "Delays in response" is presumably the other stakeholder's, but the paper does not say. Table 8 is titled "Risk evaluation criteria", the same as Tables 6 and 7 (title errors in the print).
- Eq. 3 defines n as "number of respondents" but the weights reported fit a 36-factor ranking; stated as an inconsistency in the text, not recomputed here.
- Figure 2 affiliation percentages sum to 108.2%.
- Author-stated limitation: access to data during COVID-19 (p.15).
- Stage 1 external group reduced from 8 to 3, so little external-risk information remains.
- A published-version draft mention "Journal of Risk and Financial Management 14, x FOR PEER REVIEW ... of 16" is embedded in the page text of Figures 1-3, i.e. the figures are pasted from a review draft; no impact on content.
