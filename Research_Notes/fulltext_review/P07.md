## 3 Study design
- Type: expert-questionnaire survey of cost-overrun risks, then machine-learning (Bayesian network classifier, Naive Bayes, decision tree) in WEKA. Topic is COST overrun, not schedule delay or productivity.
- Country/setting: Iran, government construction projects in Zanjan province (p.8). Authors state results generalize to government construction projects in Zanjan province.
- Risk list: 43 cost overrun risks from a literature review of seven papers (p.8), 9 source groups (Managerial, Materials and Equipment, Workforce, Financial, Project, Owner, Contractor, Consultant, Environment).
- Respondents: 41 experts took part (one refused); 2 excluded (no construction experience / high missing data); n = 39 usable (p.8). Profile (Table 2, p.9, n = 39):
  - Organization: Employer 10 (25.6%); Consultant 9 (23.1%); Contractor 11 (28.2%); Consultant/Employer 3 (7.7%); Consultant/Contractor 4 (10.3%); Government supervisor 2 (5.1%)
  - Experience (years), printed as: =<20 13 (33.3%); =<15 12 (30.8%); =<10 11 (28.2%); =<5 3 (7.7%)
  - Discipline: Civil Engineering 33 (84.6%); Architecture 3 (7.7%); Electrical Engineering 2 (5.1%); Industrial Engineering 1 (2.6%)
  - Education: Bachelor's 20 (51.3%); Master's 17 (43.6%); Ph.D. 2 (5.1%)
- Scale: 1-5 Likert for probability and for impact (1 very low, 2 low, 3 medium, 4 high, 5 very high). Experts picked randomly by visiting the organizations.
- Analysis: Cronbach alpha = 0.92 on the 43 factors (p.11); risk magnitude RM1 = Probability x Impact per response (Eq. 2, ISO 31000:2018 cited), RM2 = sum(RM1)/n with n = 43 (Eq. 3); values converted to nominal classes using a 5-class matrix (input, Fig. 2) and 3-class matrix (output, Fig. 3); CFS feature selection (step 1, 8 features retained per Table 1 on p.3); 18 Bayesian network classifiers (K2 / hill-climbing / tabu; initAsNaiveBayes or General; max parents 2, 3, None), Naive Bayes, J48 decision tree; 10-fold CV; metrics accuracy, area under ROC, precision, recall, F1.

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Direct relevance is limited: the paper is about cost overrun on Iranian government projects (Zanjan province), whole-project level, with no brick masonry, no labour productivity, no schedule delay measure and no Indian data.
- Useful as a documented method example: a 43-item literature-derived risk list scored by experts on 1-5 probability and 1-5 impact, with risk magnitude = probability x impact (ISO 31000:2018), Cronbach alpha (0.92) reliability check, and a 5x5 risk-matrix classification. This is the same probability x impact logic the project's register and matrix use.
- Table 3 gives mean probability/impact for factors that overlap with masonry-related themes (material price, material shortage, labour shortage, lack of skilled personnel, lack of knowledge and experience, poor planning and scheduling, construction quality, bad weather, site safety, owner payment delays). These values are for Iranian whole-project cost context only and should not be used as Indian masonry values.
- The Bayesian network ranking (Table 8) counts links between risks, not severity; it should not be cited as an importance ranking comparable to RII.
- The ML models predict a project-level cost class from 39 expert questionnaires; not a field-level masonry tool.

## 6 Limitations
- Very small sample (n = 39) from one province, government projects only; authors themselves restrict generalization to that setting (p.8).
- Subjective expert scores, no objective project data; class label has no "Low" instances (0 of 39), only Moderate 15 and High 24.
- Cost overrun only, not delay or productivity.
- Bayesian network links reflect correlations in 39 responses, not causation (authors acknowledge correlation vs causation, p.19-20).
- Accuracy comparison against other studies is on different datasets (authors say it does not imply superiority, p.19).
- Internal inconsistencies as printed (K2GN-Un 80.25 vs 79.75; Gondia 51.2% vs 78.4%; Ow6 impact 2.13 with probability 4.08). Experience categories printed with "=<" thresholds, unclear whether cumulative or bands.
- Novelty claims ("first time" use of BN classifier in construction management, p.20) are the authors' own and not verified here.
