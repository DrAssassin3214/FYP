## 3 Study design
- Type: questionnaire survey; Relative Importance Index (RII) ranking; Spearman and Pearson correlations between respondent groups (pp.6-7).
- Country/setting: Brasilia, Federal District, Brazil. Private companies in vertical commercial and residential buildings of medium to high standard (p.6).
- Sampling: population 129 companies (Sinduscon-DF and phone books); sample size by Stevenson (1981) = 41 companies (p.7). p.6 says 47 professionals from 40 private companies. p.7 says 51 professionals answered the in-person interview. Table 2 totals 47.
- n = 47 respondents (abstract, p.1; Table 2, p.8).
- Respondents (Table 2, p.8): Director 2, Supervisor 7, Project Manager 2, Field Manager 21, Resident Engineer 15 = 47. Experience overall: 0-5 yrs 27%, 6-10 yrs 32%, 11-15 yrs 11%, 16-20 yrs 9%, >20 yrs 21%.
- Factors: 24, taken from the list of Feitoza (2014) and compared with the literature; grouped into manpower, management, project, external factors (p.6).
- Scale: 1 minimal, 2 weak, 3 strong, 4 maximum influence on delay (p.6). RII (%) formula = weighted sum of n1..n4 divided by 4 x (n1+n2+n3+n4) x 100 (Equation 1, p.6; garbled in extraction, so the exact typeset form is not confirmed).
- Significance: 95% level, p>0.05 discarded (p.7).
- Company profile (p.7): 63.7% had more than 30 years of experience; 46% medium size; 39% running more than four projects; 76% had quality certification.

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Not masonry-specific and not Indian; the paper treats whole building projects (vertical residential/commercial buildings) in Brazil. It gives no brick-masonry or activity-level factors.
- Usable as a supporting/validation source: the top-ranked factors (rework, low labor productivity, lack of skilled labor, low labor qualification) are labour factors, and the paper's own conclusion stresses the workforce (p.13). These agree in kind with labour and productivity risks one would list for a masonry activity, but the paper does not show this for masonry.
- Material delay (rank 9 delivery, rank 13 ordering, rank 15 on-site handling), unrealistic schedule, design changes, weather (rank 19, 47.5%) and equipment appear as general-project delay causes.
- The RII method and 1-4 scale are a comparable survey design to what a masonry-risk questionnaire could use.
- Must not be read as evidence about Indian masonry; use only as an international comparison of project-level delay causes.

## 6 Limitations
- Delay causes judged by managers (directors, supervisors, project managers, field managers, resident engineers), not by masons or site labour.
- Small and uneven sample: 47 respondents, with only 2 directors and 2 project managers; 21 field managers.
- Perception-based survey; no measured delay or productivity data.
- Single city, private medium/high-standard vertical buildings; the authors note Brazil's regional variation hinders generalisation (pp.2-3).
- Factor list was fixed in advance from Feitoza (2014) (24 factors); no open-ended factors reported.
- Internal inconsistencies in the sample count (47 vs 51 professionals; 40 vs 41 companies) and citation (e5194 vs e5120).
- Table 4 group RII not tied to a stated aggregation method (NC how computed).
- Group "Manpower" includes rework and "Poor qualification in the contractor's technical staff" (group assignment as printed).
