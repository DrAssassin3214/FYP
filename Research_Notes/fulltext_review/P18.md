## 3 Study design
- Type: questionnaire survey (importance ranking) plus a simple linear regression on project records.
- Country: Saudi Arabia (abstract and Section 3). Author is affiliated in Palestine. Text elsewhere says "in Palestine and other developed and developing countries" (abstract).
- Questionnaire: 14 time-overrun (delay) factors and 13 labour-productivity factors, taken from earlier studies and local experts in residential projects. Respondents could add factors.
- Scale: ordinal 5-point (5 very high, 4 high, 3 moderate, 2 little, 1 very little).
- Sample: 50 questionnaires to contractors and 50 to consultants, chosen from an available list. 84 returned (84%): 46 contractors (92%), 38 consultants (76%). Average experience more than 10 years in building projects. Abstract says "Fifty contractors consultants" (as printed).
- Respondent positions (Fig. 1, p.163, percentages): office engineers 13.6%, site engineers 26.5%, designers 16.3%, construction managers 18.2%, project managers 12.8%, others 12.6%.
- Analysis: Excel; Importance Index (%) = sum of a(n/N)*100/5 (Eq. 1); Spearman rank correlation between contractor and consultant rankings (Eq. 2); linear regression Y = alpha + beta X (Eq. 3), Y = delay (% of planned duration), X = labour productivity.
- Regression data: 34 residential building projects in Saudi Arabia, 2018-2020, from contractors' records; time overrun and labour productivity in ceramic works and brick works (p.164). Floors: 2 floors 31%, 3 floors 37%, 4 floors 32%. Projects per year: 2018 35%, 2019 35%, 2020 30%.

### Spearman rank correlation (contractors vs consultants) (p.164, p.165)
- Delay factors rs = 0.85 (p.164 and p.165).
- Labour productivity factors rs = 0.82 (p.164, Section 4.3) but rs = 0.72 (p.165, conclusion). The paper is internally inconsistent; both printed.

- R Square 0.75; Observations 34; F 152.31.
- Intercept 142.53, t Stat 5.21, P-value 0.00.

- Brick works: the text says data covered "ceramic works and bricks works" but only a ceramic-works model is reported. No brick-works regression, productivity value or table appears anywhere in the paper.

### Recommendations (p.165)
Continuous training for labour and managers; owners to pay progress payments on time; check contractor qualifications before award and improve lowest-price bidding.

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Gives a ranked list of 14 delay factors and 13 labour-productivity factors with Importance Index values, usable as one source of candidate risk/cause items (for example labour experience, payment delay, rework, change orders, material shortage, low wages, weather, site management).
- Method (5-point scale, Importance Index, contractor vs consultant Spearman check) matches the RII-style approach in the project and can be cited as a method precedent.
- Shows an inverse delay-productivity link for ceramic (tiling) finishing work in Saudi residential buildings. It does not give a brick-masonry model.
- Setting is Saudi Arabia residential buildings, not India. Factors such as the Saudi manpower tax/limits (p.163) are context-specific. Use only as supporting literature, not as Indian evidence.

## 6 Limitations
- Country is Saudi Arabia, author affiliation Palestine; no Indian data.
- Sample 84 respondents from an "available list" (non-random); no sampling-size justification given.
- Factors are general building factors, not masonry-specific; brick-works data were collected but no brick-works result is reported.
- Regression: single predictor, 34 projects, ceramic works only; productivity and delay measurement definitions are not detailed. Table 3 is internally odd: F = 152.31 does not match t = 5.98 for a one-predictor model (t squared would equal F), and the F(1,33) degrees of freedom do not match n = 34 observations (F(1,32) expected). Reported as printed, not corrected.
- Inconsistencies: rs for productivity 0.82 vs 0.72; text says "top 13 factors" when it means the top five; Fig. 2 axis labels appear swapped/mistyped ("Labro productivity"; the y-axis label reads time overrun, x-axis labour productivity); Section 4.4 mentions "Number of floors" and "number of projects per year" as considerations.
- Importance Index is perception-based; no reliability coefficient (such as Cronbach alpha) reported; reliability is claimed only from Spearman agreement.
- Causal wording ("poor productivity causes delay") rests on a correlation.
