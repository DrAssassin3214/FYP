## 3 Study design
- Type: methodological proposal plus single case-study application; questionnaire-driven expert elicitation feeding Monte Carlo simulation (MCS). Not a survey of many respondents.
- Country/setting: Greece; renovation of a 5-star hotel, specifically a swimming pool complex with supporting constructions (engine room, rainwater drainage) and surrounding plant area (p.6).
- n: 1 expert respondent (project risk manager, initials "N.K."); 60 task IDs in the schedule (Table 1; 58 simulated activities in Table 7 - IDs 1, 2 and 5 have no duration, appear to be summary tasks; not stated explicitly - NR).
- Questionnaire (p.4, 10-12): Part 1 manager experience (years classes 0-5, 6-10, 11-15, 16-25, >25; EI = 0.2, 0.15, 0.1, 0.05, 0.0 as printed in Table 3); Part 2 fifteen general risk questions (probability and impact each on 5-point scale Very Low..Very High = 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1), risk sources: company, owner, governmental, socioeconomic, environmental; adapted from El-Sayegh et al. [40] (UAE highway); Part 3 optimistic/pessimistic duration deviation per task group, -40% to +40% in 10% steps.
- Table 4 scale (PMBOK 6th ed.): Probability % / Impact on schedule %: Very Low 1-10 / 0-5; Low 11-30 / 6-10; Moderate 31-50 / 11-20; High 51-70 / 21-40; Very High >70 / >40.
- Analysis: Eq (1),(2) corrected optimistic/pessimistic deviation = E x (EI + mean(P x I)); distributions PERT, triangular, uniform; 1000 MCS iterations in VBA/Excel 2016; makespan via MS Project 2016; compared with classic PERT method; tornado sensitivity.
- Project data: start 25 Oct 2018; CPM duration 152 days (end 24 May 2019); actual duration 159 days; hard deadline 31 May 2019 (157 workdays); five working days/week, no holidays.
- Task groups (p.3, 7, Table 2): preparation-earthworks (PE), building works (BW), electrical and mechanical installations (EMI), installation of equipment (IE), completion and testing (CT). (p.4 text says "four categories" in 2.1 but five elsewhere; printed inconsistency.)

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Gives a small, reusable list of 15 generic risk questions in five source classes (company, owner, government, socioeconomic, environmental), each scored with probability x impact on a 5-level 0-1 scale (PMBOK scale in Table 4). This matches the P x I scoring idea and could be used as a template for a masonry-specific questionnaire, after adapting items.
- Shows how an expert's ratings can be turned into optimistic/pessimistic duration ranges for task groups and run through Monte Carlo; usable as a method reference for converting risk scores to delay ranges.
- It is NOT a masonry study, not in India, and has no labour productivity or brickwork data. Task groups are generic (earthworks, building works, etc.); no masonry-specific risk factors (e.g., brick supply, mortar, labour availability) were assessed beyond "resources availability issues" and "delays from suppliers/subcontractors".
- Its factor values come from one expert on one project and must not be quoted as typical importance values.

## 6 Limitations
- n = 1 expert, 1 project (authors say validation on more real cases is future work, p.17).
- No historical data; distributions chosen pragmatically; no validation against actual finish except noting actual duration 159 days (the paper does not formally compare predictions to this).
- Risk scores are subjective; the total-risk equation is a simple heuristic that scales ranges by (EI + mean P x I); the scaling's justification is not tested.
- Internal inconsistencies noted: 4 vs 5 task categories; BW pessimistic deviation 25.25% in Table 6 vs 16.67% in Table 7 for many BW tasks; text probability ranges ("less than") are vague. Pdf header says "Buildings 2020, 10, x FOR PEER REVIEW ... of 20" embedded in some pages (draft pages embedded).
- Figures 2 and 3 not inspected.
