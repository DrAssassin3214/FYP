## 3 Study design
- Type: questionnaire survey (5-point Likert, 1 very low severity to 5 very high severity) + factor analysis + simple linear regression case study (p. 121, 124-126).
- Country: West Bank, Palestine (residential buildings; case projects 2-4 floors, 2015-2020, p. 126).
- Factors listed: 17 design-quality factors and 15 schedule-delay factors, from literature and local experts (p. 121). Pilot with 3 local experts (p. 121).
- Population: 185 registered contractors (grades 1-3) and 90 consultants (p. 121). Sample-size calc (Emory 1980) gave 23 contractors and 20 consultants (Table 1, p. 122). Questionnaires distributed to 60 contractors and 40 consultants (hand, fax, email).
- Responses: 88 received (88%): 52 contractors (86.67%) and 36 consultants (90%) (p. 122). Average experience more than 10 years in residential projects.
- Respondent positions (Fig. 1, p. 122) text: office engineers 10.4%, site engineers 25.7%, designers 28.3%, construction managers 16.6%, project managers 10.4%, others 7.5%. Figure labels read 10.40, 16.60, 28.30, 25.70, 11.50, 7.50 (%) - the 11.50 vs text 10.4 for one category conflicts; see section 6.
- Analysis: SPSS; mean score ("S.I", severity index as printed in tables) for ranking; Spearman rank correlation between contractors and consultants; factor analysis retaining Eigenvalue >= 1.0; second questionnaire on the retained factors' effect on delay, scale 0 (no effect) to 4 (extreme effect) (p. 121); regression of schedule delay (%) on number of design changes using 35 projects.

### Factor analysis (p. 124-125)
Only 4 factors with Eigenvalue >= 1: delay in payments by client for design services; staff allocation for many projects at the same time; copying and modifying from previous work; tight design schedule. Cumulative variance 75.11%. 1st factor Eigenvalue 5.42; 4th (tight design schedule) Eigenvalue 1.31. (Eigenvalues of 2nd and 3rd not printed.)

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- The paper is about residential building projects in the West Bank, not masonry specifically and not India; it does not isolate brickwork.
- Usable as supporting literature for delay-cause items that also apply to masonry: poor labour productivity (overall rank 2, S.I 4.10), lack of skilled manpower (rank 3, 4.08), rework (rank 5, 3.94), frequent change orders (rank 4, 3.96), payments delay (rank 1, 4.21), poor site management (rank 13, 3.19), effects of weather (rank 15, 3.02), additional work (rank 14, 3.06).
- Design-related items (design changes, mistakes in design; Table 2 rank 9, 3.48) can support a "design/drawing change" risk category that leads to masonry rework.
- Evidence supporting the link between labour skill, productivity and wages: the paper attributes skilled-labour shortage to wage differences with the Israeli market (p. 123), a context-specific explanation not transferable to India without further evidence.
- Its scale is a 5-point severity mean ("S.I"), not an RII formula; do not call these values RII. Table 4's "relative index" is on a different 0-4 basis.

## 6 Limitations
- Context: Palestine, small sample (88 respondents; 52 contractors, 36 consultants); respondents selected from an available list; response not obtained from labourers/masons or site supervisors in the masonry sense.
- Perception-based ranking; no productivity measurement.
- Internal inconsistencies: abstract says regression on 36 building projects, text/Table 5 say 35; Equation (3) prints 2.83 while Fig. 2/Table 5 give 2.38; Fig. 1 label 11.50% versus text 10.4% (percentages in the text sum to 98.9, labels sum to 100); Table 1 sample-size iteration n0..n7 printed but the narrative says final 23 and 20 whereas the table's n6 column values are 23 and 20 and n7 are 20 and 18; the text's d = 0.01 and t = 2 are as printed.
- The paper uses "design changes" count as the sole proxy for design quality; the regression is a single-variable model with no controls, so it shows association only.
- Table 2 and 3 headings use "S.I" but the formula for it is not given; it appears to be the mean Likert score (stated "Average score", p. 121).
- Table 4's calculation of "relative index" is not explained.
