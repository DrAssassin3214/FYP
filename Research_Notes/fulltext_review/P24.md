## 3 Study design
- Type: mixed qualitative + quantitative; Delphi-type expert validation (2 rounds) of literature-derived risk indicators, FAST diagram (value engineering), then FMEA (RPN) on a single case study. p.4, p.7.
- Country: Indonesia. Case: "XYZ Shopping Mall", Bekasi City; 23 hectares land, building area printed "160,000 sum" (sic), four floors + one basement; phase 1 completed 2013, US$48.5 million; phase 2 > US$60.6 million, completed "in 2025". p.7.
- Initial variables/indicators from literature: six variables (X1 Force majeure, X2 Construction management, X3 Internal technical, X4 Internal non-technical, X5 Human resources, X6 Execution process) and 50 risk indicators (p.4; Table 2 p.6). Dependent variable Y = additional cost and delay. p.5.
- Delphi/validation respondents: n = 11 experts (3 project managers, 1 site manager, 2 structural engineers, 5 engineering academics; text p.9). Table 6 (p.10) lists: 5 general-contractor staff (2 Project Manager, 1 Site Manager, 2 Engineer), 1 developer Project Manager, 5 academics (4 civil-engineering lecturers, 1 architecture lecturer). Education: 5 Bachelor, 4 Master, 2 Doctor. Experience bands 21-30 yrs (experts 1, 4) and 11-20 yrs (others). Text says all have >10 years.
- Validation format: binary agree/disagree per indicator; reliability by Fleiss Kappa (N = 50 items, k = 2). p.4-5.
- FMEA stage: 5 practitioner experts (Table 6 experts 1-5) brainstormed in one place to assign Severity, Occurrence, Detection on 1-10 scale; RPN = S x O x D (range 1-1000). Expert criteria: bachelor's in civil engineering, minimum 10 years in building construction, project-leader experience. p.7, p.12.
- Scale: 1-10 for S, O, D (Table 3 p.8). RPN categories Table 4 p.8.
- Not a questionnaire survey of a large sample; no RII, no Likert frequency/impact survey.

### Case-study numbers (p.15-16, p.16)
- Pile driving ran September 2023 to April 2024; original contract completion 31 January 2024.
- Hammer piling plan: 240 days, 19 piles/day (one hammer). HSPD (hydraulic static pile driver): 135 days, 34 piles/day.
- Foundation cost ~10% of total construction value, ~US$6.1 million. Potential delay if hammer kept: 110 days (text p.15); penalty ~US$671 thousand, "penalty of 1 per mile of the contract value per day" (sic, presumably per mille).
- HSPD rental/operation about 25% higher than hammer: US$636 thousand vs US$509 thousand (p.15); conclusion: increase US$127 thousand (25%), net saving up to US$544,000 (p.16).
- Conclusion states time saving 120 days (p.16). Internal inconsistency: 240-135 = 105 days; text elsewhere 110 days; conclusion 120 days. Flag.
- Low labour performance: actual achievement 19 or 24 piles/day against HSPD capacity of 34/day (p.15). Causes per experts: excessive breaks in 8-hour day, lack of induction and direction, lack of regular monitoring. Mitigation: daily induction, briefing, daily targets, supervisor monitoring of daily results (p.15-16).
- Lack of professional staff causes: recruitment screening missed soft/hard skills, differing professional culture, late shop drawings from main contractor because staff handled several projects' drawings. Mitigations: soft/hard skill tests, senior guidance of juniors, training (p.16).
- Flood mitigation: perimeter gutters to sump pits and pumps to city channels, more pumps/sump pits, deep dewatering/semi-dewatering, collaboration with national weather agency (p.15-16).
- Risk owner split by RPN class: High = top management; Medium and Low = "Messo Level"/managers and supervisors, and field technical team (p.13).

Supporting functions: manage project risks (planning, intensive monitoring); understand work method (SOP, regulation); create project team (professional hiring, capacity building); define project risks (collect data, analyse risks). ## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Direct masonry content: none. The paper is on a shopping mall in Indonesia; the only activity-level data are pile driving and basement flooding. Brickwork or blockwork is not mentioned (checked by full-text read).
- Usable as methodological reference: shows a way to move from a literature long list (50 indicators, 6 groups) to a validated shorter list (Delphi, 2 rounds) and then ranking by FMEA RPN (S x O x D, 1-10 scales, High >=192 / Medium 65-191 / Low <=64). Could be cited as one alternative to RII for ranking, with its thresholds quoted as theirs.
- Directional support (single project, 5 experts): low labour productivity (RPN 210), lack of professional/skilled staff (192), lack of workers (84) rank high or medium for a Indonesian building project; material loss (105), material quality mismatch (75), design changes (84), additional work (84) rank medium. Can be cited as supporting that human-resource and material factors appear in building-project risk lists, not as evidence for masonry in India.
- Some indicator wording (low labour productivity, lack of skilled workers, material delay/loss, drawing-reading errors, design changes) could be compared against the masonry risk register, with the caveat that this is a different trade and country.

## 6 Limitations
- Single case, single country, one building type; severity/occurrence/detection scores come from a 5-person practitioner brainstorm, so RPNs are judgmental and not statistically generalisable.
- Delphi stage n = 11 (below the 10-25 the authors cite as lower bound is borderline; they say 10-20). Fleiss Kappa 0.42 is only "fair to good". Binary agree/disagree, not ranked.
- RPN limitations acknowledged only partly; multiplicative scores with different S/O/D combos give equal RPN (e.g., many 30s and 36s); the authors added Fig 6 to compensate.
- Numerical inconsistencies: 50 - 12 + 3 = 41 vs reported 38 indicators; delay 110 days vs 240-135 = 105 vs conclusion 120 days; "160,000 sum", "penalty of 1 per mile", "Hotel development project" (p.5, likely editing residue), "Equation 1" cited for RPN which is Eq 6 (p.7); reference DOIs for several items look malformed (e.g., 10.1016/j.bb.2024.01.017) - not verified by me.
- Text claims "Previous research in India has shown FMEA superior to PMI approach" citing Lo & Liou 2018 (a Taiwan-affiliated paper); treat that attribution as the paper's claim only.
- The Table 8 does not list all 38 retained indicators' text names; the 3 added indicators have no RPN rows in Table 8 (codes not given).
- Figures 1-6 not machine-readable; Table 9 supplementary missing.
