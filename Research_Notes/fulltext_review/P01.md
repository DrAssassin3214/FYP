## 3 Study design
- Type: quantitative; questionnaire plus historical project records; classification model (Random Forest vs Random Forest optimised by Genetic Algorithm, RF-GA) to predict delay class.
- Country/setting: Diyala city, Iraq; 40 completed construction projects with different degrees of time overrun (p6). Project type (building/road etc.) is not stated (NR).
- Factor identification: literature survey, categorised into 8 sources; interviews with 15 experts to confirm (p4-5).
- Data sources: contract documents, specifications, change-order records, schedule baselines, plus questionnaire (p6).
- Questionnaire: each form = one project + 9 variables: delay level + 8 risk delay sources. Each source scored on probability and impact; overall risk impact = probability x impact (p6). Five-point Likert (very low to very high) (p6).
- Pilot: 40 parties; Cronbach's alpha 91.8% (p6).
- Respondents: questionnaire "distributed to 300 experts who worked in the collected projects" - client, engineer and other experts (p6, p12). Number of returned responses: NR.
- Output classes: <50% delay, 50%-100% delay, >100% delay (p6).
- Split: 70% (28 projects) training, 30% (12 projects) testing (p6).
- Analysis methods: RF, RF-GA (GA tunes number of trees, minimum gain, maximum tree depth); precision, sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, classification error, kappa (p3-7). GA/RF parameter values actually found: NR.

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Provides a 37-item list of delay factors in 8 source groups (Table 1) that can be a checklist for general delay-risk identification; the items are generic project-level factors, none specific to brickwork or masonry activity.
- Shows the probability x impact scoring scheme with Table 2 numeric scales (0.1-0.9 probability; 0.05-0.8 impact) - a usable example of a P x I scale; the scale values are from the paper, not validated for India.
- Labor items (7.1 poor labor productivity, 7.2 inadequacy of workforce, 7.3 lack of labor), material items (5.1-5.4) and 3.4 rework are the nearest analogues to masonry-activity risks; no values are given for them individually.
- The ML model (RF-GA) is not a rule-based tool and predicts project-level delay class; it is only loosely relevant to a rule/matrix risk register. Its factor importances are not reported.
- Setting is Iraq (Diyala); external factors such as political situation and terrorism reflect that context and should not be transferred to India without checking.

## 6 Limitations
- Only 40 projects (28 train / 12 test); test accuracy rests on 12 cases; 91.67% = 11/12. No cross-validation or confidence intervals reported.
- Single city (Diyala, Iraq); project types not described.
- No ranking or per-factor scores for the 37 factors; Fig 4 values only shown graphically at source-group level.
- Paper says questionnaire sent to 300 experts, but pilot used 40 parties; response count not reported.
- Table inconsistencies listed in section 4 (Table 3 vs Table 6; 7.41 vs 7.14; 91.76 vs 91.67).
- Comparison with other studies (Table 7) uses different datasets, so the claim of exceeding all literature is not like-for-like (authors' claim p11).
- Hyperparameters found by GA not reported; no data availability statement seen.
