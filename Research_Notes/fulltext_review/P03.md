## 3 Study design
- Type: data-driven ML framework (not a survey). Uses business process management (BPM) document-workflow data; bottleneck classification, anomaly detection, clustering, with XAI (SHAP).
- Country/setting: A465 road project, Sections 5 and 6 (Herefordshire, England to Llandarcy near Swansea, South Wales); 6 grade-separated interchanges, 29 bridges, 18 retaining walls, 3 footbridges (p5). Data are construction DOCUMENTS (drawings, schedules, models), not site activities.
- Data: six datasets; main one 3160 rows, 47 features; File-type datasets: Detail 243 rows x 26 cols; Drawing 10,094 rows; 2D Model 1103 rows; 3D Model 1658 rows; Schedule 1478 rows x 19 cols (pp5-6). Final cleaned: 2952 observations, 22 variables (12 numerical, 10 categorical) (p6); after removing rows with missing values 1970 rows x 22 columns (p8). Numerical dataset 13 variables x 1970; mixed dataset 16 variables x 1970 (p9).
- Respondents/scale: none (no questionnaire, no Likert scale).
- Methods: decision tree; Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost with grid search; SHAP; anomaly ensemble (LOF, Isolation Forest, One-class SVM, One-class SVM with SGD; outlier if flagged by at least 2 methods, and in both original and PCA data); PCA (8 variables ~90% variance); clustering (k-means, agglomerative, spectral; Gower, k-Prototypes, LSH-k-representatives); elbow method; internal validation metrics only.
- Target: bottleneck = 'Days review programme' (last stage of preliminary phase to first stage of contractual phase) greater than 45 days per the Design Quality Plan (company-specific) (p10).
- Funding: EU Horizon Europe project 101058541 Digichecks.

### Anomaly detection (pp14-15)
- Ensemble outliers: 195 (original data), 181 (PCA data), 119 overlap = 6% of 1970. Best model LightGBM F1 0.84 (p15); Figure 6 caption says CatBoost. Days review programme = 0 occurs 137 times, only one anomaly in that subset. ### Clustering (pp16-20)
- Best: agglomerative, 3 clusters, ward linkage; silhouette 0.41; Davies-Bouldin 1.24; Calinski-Harabasz 835.82 (p16). Elbow range 3 to 6 clusters.
- Cluster decision tree F1 0.98, four rules; root Volume1_S; 481 of 483 correct at that node (99.6%) (p18). LightGBM 100% accuracy on 80% training sample (p18).
- Anomaly share by cluster: Cluster 0 11.23%; Cluster 2 7.59%; Cluster 1 2.67% (p20).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Low direct relevance. It is a document-workflow (BPM) ML study of a UK highway project; the unit is design documents, not masonry site activity. No delay-cause list, no RII, no probability x impact, no labour productivity data.
- Possibly useful only as: (a) an example of rule-based, explainable (decision tree, 3 rules) risk flagging against a threshold (45 days) - similar in spirit to a rule/threshold risk tool; (b) background on delay prevalence quoted in the introduction ("more than 70%" of projects delayed, p1; citing other works, not original evidence); (c) an example of reporting limitations of data-driven tools.
- Its numbers must not be used as masonry risk evidence. Transfer to India or masonry is the authors' claim (p21 says findings transferable), not demonstrated here.

## 6 Limitations
- Authors (p21): single organisational dataset; data quality / inconsistent metadata; BPM data only (no BIM/ERP); not tested in real time or cloud; data governance and scalability not fully addressed.
- Reviewer notes: data not publicly available (Data Availability, p22); no external validation of clusters; bottleneck F1 about 0.73 with 368 false negatives; threshold of 45 days is company-specific; rows with missing values were dropped (2952 to 1970); some inconsistencies in the text (LightGBM vs CatBoost for Figure 6; the "smaller/larger" wording at the cluster tree root p18).
