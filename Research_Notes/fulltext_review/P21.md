## 3 Study design
- Type: two-stage questionnaire survey (stage 1 ranking of factors; stage 2 impact of significant rework causes on productivity) plus linear regression on project records.
- Country: Palestine (West Bank).
- Questionnaire: 3 sections (respondent/company info; factors affecting labor productivity; rework causes). 18 labor-productivity factors and 20 rework causes, from literature and local experts (p.3). 5-point ordinal scale: 1 very low, 2 low, 3 medium, 4 high, 5 very high impact. Draft pre-tested by local experts, slight changes (p.3).
- Population: 212 registered contractors (grades 1, 2, 3; Palestinian Contractors Union) and 106 registered consultants (Engineering Association, West Bank) (p.3). Sample-size formula n = (ts/d)^2/[1+(ts/d)^2/N] (Emory 1980), t = 2, d = 0.01, s from P = q = 0.5 (p.4).
- Sample: minimum 20 contractors and 17 consultants required; questionnaire sent randomly to 50 contractors and 40 consultants; 38 contractors (76%) and 30 consultants (75%) responded (n = 68 total, my sum). Respondents experienced managers, engineers, planners; average experience greater than 10 years (p.4).
- Methods: mean score ranking in Excel; factor analysis with eigenvalue >= 1 retained; Spearman rank correlation between contractor and consultant ranks; simple linear regression (p.4-5).
- Regression data: 40 building projects, West Bank, 2015-2018, from contracting firms; records of rework cost and labor productivity for plastering, ceramic works and brick works (p.4). Productivity expressed for a 2-labour crew in m2/day (p.8).
- Note: the paper does not compute RII for stage 1; it uses mean score. A "Relative index" appears only in Table 4.

Conclusion list (p.10): top 5 productivity factors = lack of labor experience, payments delay, rework due to labor mistakes, lack of supervisor's experience, materials shortage. Top 5 rework causes = lack of manpower skills, non-compliance with specifications, frequent change orders, inadequate job planning, inadequate coordination and integration. Recommendations: worker skill workshops, clear specifications, early-phase coordination, timely payments, well-defined job plans.

Literature items in text (pp.2-3, reported by this paper, not its own data): rework cost for new buildings about 5.06% of contract value (Oyewobi et al. 2011); up to 23% (Barber et al. 2000); rework increased cost of work elements 3% to 30% and original durations 10% to 77% (Wasfy 2010); labour 30-50% of project cost (Liu & Ballard 2008).

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Provides a ready list of 18 labour-productivity factors and 20 rework causes that can seed a risk checklist for brick masonry (e.g. lack of labour skill, payment delay, material shortage, supervision, coordination, change orders, specification non-compliance, job planning).
- Includes one brick-works-specific quantitative relation (Palestine, West Bank): productivity in m2/day versus rework cost %. This supports listing "rework" as a risk to masonry productivity, but the coefficients are local to that data set.
- Gives a worked example of mean-score ranking, Spearman agreement and factor analysis; it does not use RII for stage 1, so it is a method comparison, not an RII source.
- No Indian data. Rankings are Palestinian contractor/consultant perceptions; they should be treated only as candidate risk items to be checked with Indian respondents.

## 6 Limitations
- Setting is Palestine, not India; masonry not the focus (building projects overall; brick works is one of three finishing activities).
- Sample 38 contractors + 30 consultants; no labourers/site supervisors surveyed.
- Regression: 40 projects, single predictor, no controls (crew size, material, weather); R2 values are high but causality is not tested; no data on how rework cost and productivity were measured or normalised; the "40 observations" are described both as 40 projects for each of three activities.
- Ranking by mean score only; no RII or standard deviations reported; no significance test beyond Spearman.
- Internal inconsistencies: Table 1 consultants n0 = 102 vs 106 in text; Equation 3 defines Y as rework cost and X as productivity while Equations 4-6 use Y as productivity and X as rework cost; "Table 4" label used three times (Table 4 impact table, plastering regression, ceramic regression); text says 5 rework-cause checks "80%" etc. not reproduced exactly by table columns (high + extreme gives different values - not computed here); text on p.5 says 5-point scale was used for "ranking."
- Figures 1-3 not visually inspected.
