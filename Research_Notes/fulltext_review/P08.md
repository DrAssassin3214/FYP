## 3 Study design
- Type: single-case comparative study of two methods (risk matrix P x I vs Monte Carlo simulation) on the same risk inputs. Not a survey.
- Country/setting: Poland; one 53-floor, 230 m high-rise office building in central Warsaw, over 70,000 m2 office space, four underground floors, estimated total construction cost PLN 2 billion (p10-11).
- n: 12 selected risks (10 threats, 2 opportunities). Initial list came from expert interviews, historical project documentation review and brainstorming with the project management team (p7); size of initial list and number of experts: NR.
- Respondents: no questionnaire; inputs from technical documentation, tender cost estimates, benchmark investments and analysts' know-how (p13-14).
- Scales (p7): probability 5 = Very high 85-100%, 4 = High 60-84%, 3 = Moderate 35-59%, 2 = Low 15-34%, 1 = Negligible 0-14%. Impact (cost): 5 = >PLN 1,000,001; 4 = PLN 650,001-1,000,000; 3 = PLN 350,001-650,000; 2 = PLN 150,001-350,000; 1 = PLN 0-150,000. Priority = P x I (ordinal).
- Analysis: risk matrix (priority and expected exposure = impact x probability); Monte Carlo with triangular distribution for cost values (min/most probable/max) and uniform distribution for occurrence probability; 200,000 simulations (p18); risks assumed independent (p26).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Shows a P x I scoring (1-5 ordinal scales, priority = P x I) and a 12-item risk register with descriptions; this matches the probability x impact approach in the project scope as a method reference.
- Useful as a worked example of how a risk register, probability bands and cost-impact bands are laid out and then compared with a probabilistic method.
- The authors' own method list in the risk-identification step mentions delays in material delivery, weather, hiring qualified workers and geological conditions (p5), but their case register itself is not about masonry.
- Not directly transferable: the register is a Polish high-rise (concrete/steel/facade) with PLN cost bands; no masonry, no labour productivity, no delay-cause ranking, no Indian data. Use only as a method precedent, not for masonry factor values.

## 6 Limitations
- Authors state: expert/benchmark judgment (subjectivity); only 12 risks; single case, limited generalisability; static matrix; Monte Carlo assumes independent risks (p22, 25-26).
- Ordinal P x I used as semi-quantitative (p7).
- Internal inconsistencies in printed tables (see section 4, Table 7). Impact scale in cost terms only (PLN); scale bands are project-specific.
- Claim that Monte Carlo is "more accurate" is asserted, not tested against actual outcomes; risks are cost items not schedule items.
