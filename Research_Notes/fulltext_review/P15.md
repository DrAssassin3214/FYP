## 3 Study design
- Type: two-stage questionnaire survey; stage 1 literature review (11 journals, Table 1) giving 23 factors; stage 2 survey of site management on mitigation measures (p3).
- Country/place: Indonesia, East Java Province, several cities (p3, p9).
- Stage 1: n = 83 construction workers; questions read aloud in Indonesian or Javanese and recorded by research team (p3).
- Stage 2: site engineers, supervisors, project managers, other field management personnel; sample size NR (p3, p7).
- Scale: Likert 1-4 (1 no impact, 2 low, 3 moderate, 4 high impact) (p3). Stage 2 also 1-4 (p3).
- Method: RII = sum W / (A x N), A = 4, N = 83 (p5). Printed interpretation on 0-100 scale: >67 highly influential, 33-67 moderate, <33 little (p3); table values are on 0-1 scale.
- Reliability: Cronbach's alpha 0.955307; standardised-item alpha 0.983245; 23 items (Table 3, p5). Text says "0.95" and cites 0.8-0.9 as good.
- Activity-specific? No; general construction workers, trade not stated.

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Gives worker-perception RII values for 23 generic labour-productivity factors; no masonry-specific data. Indonesian context, so transfer to Indian masonry gangs is indicative only.
- Useful as supporting evidence for risk categories: wages/incentives/late payment, supervision, tools, weather, fatigue, skills, communication, material delay, work method, safety.
- Worker-side (not manager-side) viewpoint is a contrast to most delay studies.

## 6 Limitations
- Single province, n = 83, no trade breakdown; authors say findings need validation elsewhere (p9).
- Only 1-4 scale; RII thresholds stated on 0-100 scale but table on 0-1 (mixed).
- Stage 2 sample size and demographics NR; Cronbach text (0.95, "0.8-0.9 good") inconsistent with its own cited range.
- Some table items ambiguous ("No consumption" is explained in discussion as unavailability of food, p7).
- Ethics "Not applicable" (p10).
