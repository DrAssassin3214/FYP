## 3 Study design
- Type: cross-sectional expert questionnaire survey, mixed-methods claimed (pragmatism, "concurrent triangulation" and "sequential explanatory" both stated, p7; interviews are mentioned on p7-9 but no interview results are reported). Analysis: EFA, PLS-SEM, then ANN (two-stage SEM-ANN).
- Country/setting: cold regions of India (stated as parts of Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim on p2; "Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand" on p8). Infrastructure/construction projects in cold regions generally; no brick masonry or specific trade.
- Factor identification: literature review gave 35 weather-related factors (Table 1); 10 experts (stated ">25 years" experience, p8) added 3 (Delays in overall project [T5], Unsafe working conditions [S6], Additional maintenance work [S9]) giving 38 (p8). Abstract says "42 delay factors ... four groups" (abstract, p1); Table 1 has 12 T + 13 R + 13 S + 4 D (delay effects) = 42 codes, 38 weather factors + 4 delay effects (my count of Table 1 codes: T1-T12, R1-R13, S1-S13, D1-D4).
- Sampling: pilot with 12 experts (>15 yrs experience); invitations to "more than 150" experts by email/phone; 83 completed surveys; stated response rate 64.6% (p10); "35% of responses invalid or insufficient" (p10).
- n = 83.
- Scale: 7-point Likert, 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree), rating influence of factors in Table 1 (p9).
- Software: SPSS v22 (EFA, varimax), SmartPLS 3 (PLS-SEM), SPSS 26 (ANN, p16).
- EFA: loadings below 0.40 discarded; T12, S13, R13 removed; 35 observed factors retained (p12).
- ANN: inputs = 3 SEM latent factors (snowfall, rainfall, temperature); output = climatic delays; one hidden layer, sigmoid activation, MLP; 90% training / 10% testing; 10 networks (p15-18).

Respondent profile (Table 2, p10): Gender Male 69 (83.13%), Female 14 (16.86%). Sector Public 35 (39.75%), Private 48 (55.42%). Experience <5 yrs 9 (10.84%), 5-10 yrs 15 (18.07%), 11-15 yrs 45 (54.21%), >15 yrs 14 (16.86%). Designation Project Manager 36 (43.37%), Engineer 22 (26.50%), Contractor 18 (21.68%), Other 7 (8.43%). Text says 71% have >11 years (p9) and 70% managerial.

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India (plain)
- Useful as a methodological example: literature-derived factor list, expert validation, 7-point Likert survey, factor-loading cut-off 0.40, a ranked list of risk factors, and reporting of respondent profile. It is an Indian survey of construction professionals.
- It is about weather (snowfall, rainfall, low temperature) in cold Himalayan regions, not about brick masonry, labour productivity of masons, or general delay causes. Snowfall and low temperature are not relevant to most Indian brick-masonry sites; rainfall relevance is partial.
- Factors that could be cross-checked against a masonry delay list by the student: material storage, transportation delay, unsafe working conditions, structural damage, task productivity (D4), worker well-being (D3), hiring and recruiting, price escalation, equipment problems. The paper gives them only in the weather context (the printed loadings/coefficients apply to weather effects, not to general delay probability or impact).
- Not a probability x impact or RII study; the ranking is by EFA factor loading and the SEM/ANN importance is for the three weather constructs only. The ANN is a prediction/importance model, not a rule-based tool; no tool for practitioners is delivered.
- Do not cite it as evidence for masonry-specific risks.

## 6 Limitations
- Authors state: small sample (n=83); limited to cold regions of India; future work should use larger samples and other regions (p21).
- Observed by this review (as printed, not corrected): abstract says 42 factors/four groups but the analysis uses 38 then 35 weather factors (42 only by counting the 4 delay-effect codes); abstract closing sentence about "clearly articulating its novel contributions" reads like a reviewer comment left in the text; "10 experts >25 years" vs pilot 12 experts >15 years; response rate 64.6% with 83 of 150 (83/150 = 55.3%) and "35% invalid" - arithmetic not reconciled by the paper; 83 of "more than 150" invited; public sector 35 = 39.75% printed (35/83 = 42.17%) while 39.75 + 55.42 does not sum to 100 (only 83 of 83 counted if 35+48 = 83); Table 4 ranks duplicated and out of range (41); Table 4 loadings differ from Table 8 coefficients; AVE of Temperature 0.425 and Snowfall 0.438 under the usual 0.5 threshold, text cites "0.04"; Table 5 chi-squared recommended "less than 2" while PLS-SEM fit indices (RMSEA, CFI, SRMR) are described mixed with CB-SEM terminology; only three inputs to the ANN, so "importance" covers only three constructs; ANN R2 stated as "72% accuracy"; ANN 10 MSE inconsistent; the response variable of the ANN and how "climatic delays" was scored is not stated; mixed-methods design claimed but no interview data reported; Likert scale described as agree/disagree ("influence") rather than frequency/severity; claims "no studies with this particular focus" (p21) and "novel" - novelty not verifiable from the paper; data available only on request.
- Many figures quoted in the discussion (e.g. 30-40% delay, 10-15%) lack a clear source.
