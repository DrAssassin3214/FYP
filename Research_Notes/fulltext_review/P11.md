## 3 Study design
- Type: single-case, ex ante risk assessment using the ANAC (Italian National Anti-Corruption Authority) probability x impact risk matrix, then monetisation of selected risks and comparison with actual costs (ex post).
- Country/setting: Italy, Province of Treviso, Vittorio Veneto; "State Road No. 51 of Alemagna Vittorio Veneto Variant" (SSv-51), a road-with-tunnel infrastructure project; integrated design-and-build contract (p.4). Estimated cost in 2017 executive project EUR 61,024,448 (p.6).
- Sample: n = 1 project. Respondents: matrix "filled out by local stakeholders with extensive knowledge of the area ... coupled with technical expertise in engineering within ... infrastructure projects" (p.10). Number of respondents: NR.
- Risk list: ANAC 4 categories, 21 risk types (RC1-RC11 Construction, RP1-RP4 Performance, RD1-RD2 Demand, RO1-RO4 Other) (Table 1, p.9).
- Scales: Probability A Quite impossible [0,1%], B Unlikely [1,25%], C Likely [25,50%], D Very likely [50,75%], E Almost certain [75,100%]. Impact on cost: 1 Light [0,5%], 2 Mediocre [5,10%], 3 Severe [10,20%], 4 Critical [20%,infinity) (p.9). Action levels (colour): MINIMUM green - evaluate improvement actions; LOW yellow - plan corrective actions medium-short term; MEDIUM orange - urgently schedule corrective actions; HIGH red - implement immediate corrective actions (p.9-10).
- Analysis: 5 phases (identify, describe, matrix, mitigation/residual risk, risk value). Risk value RVi = Cost Impact CIi x Probability Pi, summed over five increase levels; IVi uses interval midpoints (2.5%, 8.0%, 15.5%) and 50% for the open-ended top class (p.11-12).

### Headline results as printed
- Estimated risk value EUR 3,706,184 vs actual overrun on selected items EUR 4,412,830 vs contingency EUR 1,422,630 (Figure 5 caption, p.14).
- Residual unexplained overrun "only EUR 706,645", "delta ... of only 1.32%" (p.13-14); conclusion: actual overrun reduced to 1.32%, "decreasing it by 4.52%" (p.15).
- Abstract (p.1) instead prints "7.53%" ex ante increase and "differs only by 1.34%" - inconsistent with body (7.45%, 1.32%). Also body text p.13 says total costs increased "by 8.87%, with a 5.54% increase attributed to the selected costs", which is the reverse of Table 5 (5.54% total; 8.87% selected).
- Literature figures quoted (p.3-4, from refs): Flyvbjerg et al. average cost escalation rail 45%, fixed link 34%, roads 20%; Netherlands rail 10.6%, roads 18.6%, fixed link 21.7%. Italy transport spending share peak 4.5% (2007) to just above 3% (2011-2020) (p.1-2).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India (plain)
- Useful as a worked example of the probability x impact matrix with defined classes and action levels, and of recording per-risk description and mitigation (Table A2) - maps to a risk register + matrix.
- The 21-item risk list is a public-procurement/PPP list for a road project (design, eminent domain, interference, permits, finance, demand); it is not a masonry or site-activity list and gives no brick-masonry delay causes, no RII ranking, and no labour productivity data.
- Cost-based impact classes (0-5, 5-10, 10-20, >20%) would need re-basing for delay/time (authors themselves say time impact is future work, p.9-10).
- Indian context: none. Do not cite as evidence on Indian masonry delays.

## 6 Limitations
- Single case (authors, p.15); ratings by unspecified local stakeholders; assumes experts can foresee probabilities.
- Only construction-phase cost overruns; no time overruns or management-phase costs.
- Percentage increase classes and the 50% top-class value are authors' choice; probabilities per level (Table 6) are expert/judgement based; method of eliciting them not detailed.
- Validation is one project, applied after actual costs were known (risk of hindsight; the paper calls results preliminary).
- Internal numeric inconsistencies noted above (abstract vs body; 8.87%/5.54% swap; 2.33% vs 2.86% contingency basis).
- Data not publicly available (ANAS permission needed).
