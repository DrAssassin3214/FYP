## 3 Study design
- Type: Mixed. (a) literature review to build a General Cause-of-Delay (CoD) list; (b) small expert questionnaire survey on one case project; (c) machine-learning text-mining model (DREAM, Delay Root causes Extraction and Analysis Model) on project Minutes of Meetings (MoM).
- Country / setting: Serbia; one case project, a highway, 2 sections (A = 28 km, B = 37 km), total 65 km, Red FIDIC contract; international contractor, domestic public employer and engineer; road open to traffic at data collection; total project delay 42% of contracted duration (p6).
- Literature step (p5-6): 74 academic articles (1997 to 2021) -> 157 CoD -> merged to 94 -> pilot study with 3 academic professors (>20 years experience, individual interviews), 3 new CoD added (2.2, 2.3, 6.4) -> General CoD List of 52 CoD in 8 categories (Appendix A). (The Conclusions on p19 say "54 CoD grouped into 8 categories"; Table A1 as printed lists 52 - printed inconsistency.)
- Survey (p6-8): respondents = 3 (one each: contractor, owner, consultant/engineer), each >20 years experience; scale = 4-point Likert 0-3 (0 no or very low impact, 1 low, 2 median, 3 high) plus "I do not know"; first version was 5-point, reduced after expert feedback. Analysis = Spearman rank correlation between stakeholder pairs (Eq. 1: rs = 1 - 6*sum(d^2)/(n^3 - n)).
- ML step (p8-16): MoM weekly; Section 1 = 64 MoM, Jan 2018 to Feb 2020, 1501 original statements; Section 2 = 62 MoM, Feb 2018 to Feb 2020, 1411 original statements (Table 4). Expert panel of 3 labelled statements to CoD and project Entity (route/bridge/tunnel/misc), 6 months, 3 rounds, consensus = 2 of 3 agree. Result: 497 CoD-related statements. Model: DistilBERT fine-tuned (Adam 5e-5, batch 2, 10 epochs, max seq length 512); PEGASUS used to generate synthetic statements in expansion experiment. Validation: 10-fold cross-validation; non-CoD (NC) class added with N = 90% of CoD statements per fold. Five experiments E1 (individual CoD), E2 (CoD group), E3 (Entity), E4 (segmented, route only), E5 (segmented + expanded).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India (plain)
- Useful as a source for a structured delay-cause checklist: the 52-item, 8-group General CoD List (e.g. 3.1 lack of laborers, 3.2 low productivity and unqualified laborers, 3.3 lack of material in market, 3.4 delay in material delivery, 3.5 inadequate quality of material, 5.1 rework, 8.7 unfavorable weather) overlaps with generic factors a masonry risk list would also contain. It is a general list, not masonry-specific, and was built from literature plus 3 professors.
- Table 1 and the survey result are a caution on method: stakeholder role strongly changes who is blamed (rs 0.26 to 0.36 among three parties), supporting the use of more than one respondent type when identifying risks.
- Table 7 shows which causes were most often recorded in site meeting minutes on this one project (1.5, 3.2, 5.1, 5.4, 8.1, 8.7 had the most instances), but this is a Serbian highway, not masonry, and counts are statement frequencies, not importance or probability x impact.
- It does not provide RII, probability x impact scoring, labour productivity data, or a rule-based risk tool. The ML approach (DistilBERT on meeting minutes) would need a labelled masonry/Indian site-minutes dataset to be reused; none is provided. The paper's own claim that DREAM "applies to other types of projects if retrained" is the authors' statement, untested here.
- Only one cited item (ref 16, Doloi et al. 2012, Indian construction projects delays) is Indian; it is cited, not analysed, in this paper.

## 6 Limitations
- Single case project (one Serbian highway); survey n = 3 (one per party); expert labelling panel n = 3, partly connected to the case project; results are not generalisable and no significance test is reported.
- Survey results per factor are not reported; only correlations.
- Low instance counts for many CoD (2 to 13 instances for 11 of the 19 rows in Table 7); recall 0 for many; only 19 CoD appear in Table 7 out of 52 in the list, and only 5 of 8 groups had any labelled statements (groups 2, 6, 7 had none in Table 6).
- E4/E5 segmentation done for route entity only; bridge and tunnel had too few instances.
- Conclusion that DREAM is "superior" to surveys rests on a comparison the authors say is "not directly comparable" (Spearman vs confusion matrix) (p17).
- Misc entity and non-CoD class absorbed many errors; "attractor class" effects acknowledged.
- Internal inconsistencies noted: 52 vs 54 CoD; 0.24 vs 0.26; "three out of top six (3.2 and 5.6)"; Table 6 note of Table 1 reference for CoD labelling; text says Section "4. Expert-Based Approach" printed twice (extraction duplicate).
- Written for large infrastructure; contract (Red FIDIC) and organisational context differ from small masonry work packages.
