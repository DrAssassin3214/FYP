## 3 Study design
- Type: theoretical / methodological study of planning (schedule) data using statistical software (R-Commander / R-Studio). Not a survey. Abstract p1: "a simulation is carried out with data that are hypothetical but consistent with real projects".
- Country / setting: authors in Spain; industrial EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction) projects: a set of power generation projects (PXE) and one non-power project (INE) (p1). Not residential or masonry.
- n: 6216 activity rows from five projects, reduced to 5145 after filtering (17.2% decrease) (pp5-6). 96 component types (p15).
- Respondents: none (no questionnaire). Data = planned vs actual dates of schedule activities.
- Scale: none (days; "weighted" variables = variable divided by project duration in months, p5).
- Variables: Delay Start, Delay Finish, Duration Difference, Delay Predecessor (days); factors System, Specialty, Discipline, Component (p5).
- Filter: Delay Finish Weighted and Duration Difference Weighted kept for -10 < X < 30 (p6).
- Methods: descriptive statistics, contingency tables, correlation matrix, simple/two-degree/multiple linear regression, one-way ANOVA, component "Index = Median + IQR" (pp6-7, 15).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Indirect only. The paper is not about masonry, not Indian, and has no risk factor list ranked by RII or probability x impact.
- Useful ideas: (a) delays propagate from predecessor to successor activities (r = 0.55; slope about 0.56; R2 0.3053), supporting a "predecessor delay / sequencing" risk in a masonry activity (e.g. delayed material supply or preceding RCC/centering work) as a hypothesis to test; (b) a median + IQR index to rank delay-prone components from schedule records, which could inspire a data-driven ranking if schedule logs exist; (c) intro p2 lists generic delay causes from literature (low productivity, material shortages, poor site management, late procurement, unreliable subcontractors, etc.) that can serve as candidate factors for a questionnaire, to be sourced from the cited originals rather than from this paper.
- It is a rule-/statistics-on-schedule approach, not an AI risk tool and not a labour productivity study.
- Not valid to transfer any numeric value (coefficients, indices) to Indian brick masonry.

## 6 Limitations (as stated and observed)
- Data hypothetical ("based on and in general consistent with real projects"); authors say some results "could be considered as inexact" and focus on feasibility of methodology, not numerical results (p18).
- Only 5 projects, heterogeneous; larger sample (379 or 68) advised (p18).
- Filters reduce data; multiple regression and second-degree successor results not conclusive (low observations, about 2.1%) (pp13, 19).
- Industrial EPC power-plant context limits extrapolation to other sectors (p19).
- Single predecessor per activity assumed (p17).
- Observed: Table 10 prints "2975.00" as a t value for an intercept of 7.99 / SE 2.68 (inconsistent; likely typo); Table 11 reports "Predecesor"; p11 text says R2 is for "Mechanical Construction activities" while Table 7 is described as all activities; Table 3 header "Duration Advance Stage / Early Stage" cannot be mapped to specific stage definitions.
- Observed: p6 text says distributions "left-skewed" while mean > median and positive skewness (Table 4) indicate right skew.
