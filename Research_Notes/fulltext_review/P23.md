## 2 Pages read
All 16 PDF pages (printed pp. 55-70). The Read tool's PDF renderer (pdftoppm) was not installed, so the text layer was extracted with python/pypdf and read in full; page images were not viewed. Figure 1 (pie chart) was read only through its text labels.

## 3 Study design
- Type: questionnaire survey (RII + factor analysis in SPSS + Cronbach alpha) followed by one field case study (time and motion study, TMS) of brick/block wall construction.
- Country/region: India; survey respondents all from Telangana State (TS) region (p.58); field site in Telangana (abstract, p.55).
- Survey: 38 LP factors in 8 groups (Table 1, p.59). 120 respondents contacted by e-mail, March 2017; 44 (36%) responded, one per firm/organisation/institute (p.58). 11% academicians (third-party consultants from educational institutes), 89% construction managers/site engineers from private, government and multinational firms. Fig. 1 (p.59): Academicians 11.4%, Government 11.4%, MNC 31.8%, Private Companies 45.5%. Inclusion: experience of at least one complete building project or minimum 5 years in India.
- Scale: 5-point Likert, 1 very low, 2 low, 3 moderate, 4 high, 5 very high; raters considered time, cost and quality. Mean of the five-point scale results 3.64 (p.58).
- RII = sum(w x)/(A N), A = 5, N = 44; factors with RII more than 0.7 retained (28 of 38) (p.61). Printed Table 3 includes a factor at exactly 0.700.
- Cronbach alpha for the 38 factors: 0.937 (p.60).
- Field study: AAC (autoclaved aerated concrete) block wall construction (p.59) in an ongoing five-storey residential apartment (p.64); 22 workers (13 masons, 9 helpers) on the activity (p.59); 24 mason-helper combinations observed, random days mid-October to mid-December 2017, at least 30 min per combination, total 2021 min and 600 sq ft (p.64). LP in min per sq ft.

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- Directly about an Indian building site and a wall-masonry (AAC block, called brick wall in the text) activity, so it supports the existence of the survey-ranked factors for a masonry risk list: unsafe working conditions, poor planning/scheduling, material shortages, payment delay, weather, skill, supervision, tools, rework, change orders.
- The only field-measured delays here are material delay (9% of time), supervision delay (3%), tools delay (2%), unskilled-work delay (1%), idle 1%, misc 1% in 24 mason-helper observations at one site. These are usable as a single-site data point, not as a general rate.
- The survey RII values are for building construction in general (not masonry-specific); only the field part is masonry-specific.
- Appropriate use: as one supporting source for factor identification and for a time-loss anchor, cited with its n (44 survey responses, 24 observations, one site).

## 6 Limitations
- Small sample: 44 responses (36% of 120), all Telangana-based, one respondent per organisation; 11% academics.
- RII computed with N = 44; the 0.7 cut-off removed 10 factors, so the ranking covers only the retained 28.
- Field study: one site, one activity (AAC block wall), 24 observations of at least 30 min each, mid-Oct to mid-Dec 2017, chosen on days with no extreme weather (so weather loss excluded by design); authors state generalisation is limited (p.66).
- Only four of the eight reorganised groups were linked to measured field delays; survey-field link is by authors' mapping, not by statistical test (abstract/conclusion mention "regression analysis" but none is presented).
- Printed inconsistencies (copied as printed, not corrected): (a) abstract says work characteristics etc. give "15% loss" and "about 11%" for work characteristics, while Table 9 gives 2 + 9 = 11 and the 15% is the whole non-productive total less 2%; (b) Table 9 gives Idle/Talking 2 whereas Table 8 gives Idle 1 and Misc 1; (c) text p.65-66 cites "Table 7" and "Table 8" for field/survey correlation (should be Tables 9/8) and "Table 5" for regrouped survey analysis (Table 5 is the rotated matrix; grouping is Table 6); (d) Table 4 skips component 9; (e) observation 1 productivity is 6 min/sft in A1 and 17 in A2 (and other rows differ, e.g. obs 3: 4 vs 8); (f) the 4% ILO allowance is stated but the deduction is 2%; (g) 22 workers on the activity vs 24 combinations observed; (h) Cronbach alpha formula as printed has sum sigma-i squared over sigma-i squared (symbols garbled in extraction); (i) Likert mean 3.64 stated without defining over which items.
- Work Conditions (0.617) and Assured and Safety Work (0.654) groups have alpha below 0.7 (the paper's own threshold, p.60, with 0.6 allowed for exploratory studies).

## 7 NR / NC items
- NR: RII and counts for the 10 factors rejected below 0.7; Likert mean per factor; respondent experience years or designation split beyond Fig. 1 percentages; N of masons vs helpers observed per combination; wall dimensions/block size; wage or rate data; any delay duration in days; confidence intervals.
- NR: statistical test linking survey groups to field delays (none printed).
- NC: none (all tables were legible in the extracted text layer; Figure 1 values read from labels only). Rendering of the Cronbach alpha equation symbols is garbled in the text layer (p.60), so the exact printed form is NC.
