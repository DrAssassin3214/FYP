## 3 Study design
- Type: single case study, exploratory ("initial exploration") of an LLM-based risk identification framework. No quantitative risk scoring.
- Country/setting: Croatia; 'Trg pravde' judicial complex, Zagreb (southern building + underground garage); public-sector, EU NPOO-funded. Source: ~606 pages, 225,826 words of technical specification from the Croatian e-procurement bulletin (p. 4).
- Data: tender technical specification (in Croatian) fed unprocessed to three general-purpose LLMs: GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (no fine-tuning). New chat per model, phase-by-phase prompts (Initiating, Planning, Executing, Closing; PM2 lifecycle). Output format: 7-column table (Code, Risk Source, Cause/Driver, Risk, Affected Area, Risk Category, Risk Bearer) in cause-risk-effect form. Filtering by category (Financial, Regulatory) and by stakeholder (Investor, General Contractor) done with GPT-5 only (p. 7).
- Respondents: 3 external experts (academia and industry; Croatia, Germany, United States); 30-item questionnaire (mixed star ratings, multiple choice, open text) (p. 6). n = 3.
- Scale: no probability/impact scale, no RII. Prioritisation explicitly NOT done by the AI (pp. 16-17).
- Analysis: four-layer validation - cross-model triangulation, comparison with literature, check against project documents, expert questionnaire (cross-comparison of respondents). No statistical tests. No ground-truth risk register was available (p. 6).

## 5 Relevance to risk identification for brick masonry in India
- Relevant only as a method precedent: shows LLMs can produce a structured risk register (cause-risk-effect, category, risk bearer) from project documents, with human expert validation. Supports a register layout (Code, Source, Cause, Risk, Affected Area, Category, Bearer) usable for a masonry activity register.
- Generic construction-phase risk themes that overlap with masonry delay causes: skilled labour shortage / subcontractor performance (RCP-15), site supervision and quality control (RCP-16), poor planning and sequencing (RCP-20), adverse weather (RCP-21), late payments (RCP-17), late scope changes (RCP-10), permit/inspection delays. These were AI-generated for a Croatian public building, not measured in masonry or India.
- No RII, no probability x impact, no labour productivity data, no brick masonry content, no Indian setting. Cannot be used as evidence of risk importance or ranking.
- The paper itself states the AI did identification and classification only; prioritisation needs human input (pp. 16-17).

## 6 Limitations
- Single project, public-sector, EU-funded, Croatia; heavy non-transferable risks (NPOO deadline, heritage, transformer stations).
- n = 3 experts; no statistics; Likert-type results only as counts.
- No ground-truth register; validation partly circular (literature "alignment", cross-model agreement).
- Only GPT-5 used for filtering tables (Tables 1-4); filtered tables list only 26 GPT-5 risks. Risk lists not ranked or scored.
- Authors' own caveats: AI output can be over-generalised, context-dependent, convincing but unverified; prioritisation consistency weak (pp. 14-18). Novelty claim "first study" is the authors' own and should not be repeated as fact.
- Preprint-style journal article but supplementary (S1-S5, Zenodo) full risk tables and questionnaire not available in the PDF.
