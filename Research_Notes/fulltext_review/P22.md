## 3 Study design
- Type: questionnaire survey, ranking by Relative Importance Index (RII); reliability by Cronbach's alpha. Cross-sectional perception survey.
- Country: India (all over India; no state or city given).
- Factor identification: literature review plus expert interviews; 56 attributes identified, shortlisted to 40 after a pilot survey (p.4). Pilot responses "around 35" (p.4).
- Questionnaire: 40 questions; reviewed by faculty/scholars; circulated by email, WhatsApp, social media, with online and offline collection and "direct on-site data collection"; QuestionPro tool mentioned; open about two weeks (p.4).
- Respondents: consultants, architects, civil contractors, developer, PMC, academia (abstract p.1). Contractors, designers, consultants, academicians (p.4).
- n: abstract p.1 says 201 valid responses, response rate 32%. Methodology p.4 says 206 valid responses, response rate "about 34%", 605 professionals contacted. Table 3 uses 201 for every factor. Internal inconsistency in the paper (see 6).
- Scale: paper says RII = sum of responses / (number of responses x maximum Likert value) (p.5) but does not print the scale points or anchors. Arithmetic: 806 / (5 x 201) = 0.80199, consistent with a 5-point scale (my check, not stated by the paper).
- Reliability: Cronbach's alpha 0.965, N of items 40 (Table 2, p.5). Abstract says "above 0.95".

## 5 Relevance to brick-masonry risk identification in India
- It is an Indian survey (p.1), so it is usable as general Indian evidence on factors affecting construction productivity: resource availability, scope clarity, design changes, rework, site availability, payment, planning, labour strike, working hours, climate.
- It is whole-industry and not about masonry or any single trade. The factors are project-level and management-level; none is specific to brick masonry.
- It ranks "productivity factors", not "delay causes"; it can only support a risk list as supporting evidence of general importance, not as masonry-specific RII.
- The aggregate item "Availability of resources" does not separate material, labour and equipment.

## 6 Limitations
- Sample size and response rate are stated two ways (201/32% in abstract; 206/34% on p.4; 605 contacted).
- Scale points/anchors not printed; respondent role breakdown, experience, and project types not given.
- Convenience distribution through email/WhatsApp/social media; no sampling frame.
- Table 3 prints "Conflict of interest among team members" twice (Sr 20 and 22, 0.737313 and 0.730348); appears to be a duplicate or mislabel.
- Results text (pp.5-6) discusses "improper supervision" and "poor site management" as main factors, but these are not items in Table 3, and the "top five" sentence on p.5 is garbled ("having the value on Rework ..."). Conclusion (p.6) refers to findings (supervision, labour motivation, labour skills shortage) not shown in the tables.
- Ties are shown with sequential Sr. No.; no tie-adjusted ranks.
- Paper's language is rough in places; interpretation text should not be relied on beyond Table 3.
- No labour productivity measurement (e.g. output per hour), only perception.
