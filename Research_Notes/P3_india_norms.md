### P3-01  IS 7272 (Part I) - 1974, North Zone   [STANDARD - labour output constants]
- Issuer: Bureau of Indian Standards (ISI). Title: "Recommendation for Labour Output Constants for Building Work, Part I: North Zone". Adopted 15 Mar 1974; Amendment No. 1 Feb 1979; reaffirmed 2010; sixth reprint Feb 2001. Cover reads "Reaffirmed 2010".
- Source read: archive.org public copy (BIS Right-to-Information release) https://archive.org/details/gov.in.is.7272.1.1974 ; PDF text extracted with pypdf, brickwork page is a JBIG2 scan with no text layer, so I read the archive.org page image (PDF p.14, printed p.8) visually.
- Scope (Sec. 1.1): Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan only (other zones "would be covered separately"). I did not find or read other parts (south, east, west zones).
- How the constants were obtained (Foreword 0.4, read in the page image): "arrived at by the Central Building Research Institute, Roorkee by taking actual observations, using work measurement techniques, on the construction sites at Delhi and Roorkee. Different types of building works up to 10 m height of CPWD, MES and other organizations ... where the workers from neighbouring states were working"; observations sufficient "to ensure a confidence limit of 95 percent and an accuracy of +/-5 percent"; relaxation and working-condition allowances per Ministry of Labour standard values; other allowances (organisation, incidental holdups, preparatory-work ineffective time, small-size-work weightage) from activity-sampling studies and committee agreement. Number of observations: NR.
- Unit: Amendment No. 1 (Feb 1979) replaces the column-5 heading with "Recommended Constant in Days (8 Working Hours)" and changes 'M3'/'M2' to 'm3'/'m2'.
- Table 1, item iv) "Brick work (Straight Walls)", printed p.8 (PDF p.14). Values are labour-days per unit:
  | Row | Work | Unit (as printed in scan) | Mason | Mazdoor | Bhisti |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | a | Brick work in walls exceeding one brick thick, in cement/lime mortar | m3 | 0.94 | 1.80 | 0.20 |
  | b | Brick work in walls, one brick thick, in cement/lime mortar | m2 | 0.25 | 0.40 | 0.10 |
  | c | Half brick walls (with or without hoop iron reinforcement) in cement mortar | m2 | 0.12 | 0.20 | 0.07 |
  | d | Tile work in super-structure in cement mortar (not standard brick; not used) | m3 | 1.80 | 1.80 | 0.20 |
  Remarks printed in col. 6: (i) constants include labour involved in scaffolding; (ii) constants can be adopted for brick work with any mix or mortar; (iii) labour for mixing mortar will be extra.
- Reading caveat: the scan's superscripts are low resolution. Rows a and d look like m3, rows b and c like m2. Rows b and c per m2 is the only physically sensible reading (0.25 mason-day per m3 would mean 4 m3 per mason-day). This is my inference, flagged.
- Derived (by me, not in source): one-brick wall 1/0.25 = 4.0 m2 per mason-day; half-brick 1/0.12 = 8.3 m2 per mason-day; walls > 1 brick 1/0.94 = 1.06 m3 per mason-day. Mazdoor : mason labour-day ratio = 1.80/0.94 = 1.9 (rows a), 0.40/0.25 = 1.6 (b), 0.20/0.12 = 1.7 (c); adding bhisti: total labour-days per mason-day = 2.9-3.1 (a), 2.4 (b), 2.3 (c) - a NORM-implied gang mix, not an observed crew.
- Evidence depth: FULL TEXT (page image). Type: standard (norm), the constants came from CBRI work measurements, but the raw observation data, SD or n are not given.
- Limitations: 1974 practice (north India, works up to 10 m height); no floor-height factor for brickwork in this part; no stated thickness in mm; no variability. IS 7272 Part 1 is a BIS priced standard - the free archive.org copy is a public-safety/RTI release; check current licence before quoting large parts. Not read: whether a later revision/other zones exist (NC).

### P3-06  Karthik and Rao (2019), Engineering Journal 23(2):55-70   [PEER-REVIEWED PAPER, India, measured (time-and-motion)]
- Title: "The Analysis of Essential Factors Responsible for Loss of Labour Productivity in Building Construction Projects in India". DOI 10.4186/ej.2019.23.2.55. Open access (held in Research_Papers/5_Masonry_Productivity_Delay). Evidence depth: FULL TEXT.
- Field study (Sec. 4.2, 5.4): five-storey residential apartment in Telangana State; wall activity with AAC blocks (the text also calls it "brick wall construction"; thickness NR); the activity involved "22 workers (13 masons & 9 helpers)"; 24 mason + helper combinations observed on random days mid-Oct to mid-Dec 2017, undisturbed periods, at least 30 minutes each; "Total time and work quantity ... 2021 minutes and 600 square feet".
- Table 8 (p.65): productive task motions 83 percent (brick laying 44, alignment 20, finishing 13, joinery installation 1, brick slicing 5, scaffold installation 1); non-productive 17 percent (material delay 9, tools delay 2, supervision delay 3, unskilled work delay 1, idle/talking 1, miscellaneous 1). - Table A1 (p.69) gives the raw data for each of the 24 observations (time in min, quantity in sft, min/sft printed as rounded integers 2-17 in Table A2 / 2-10 in A1). I transcribed it; my sums reproduce the paper's totals (2021 min, 600 sft).
- DERIVED by me from Table A1 (not printed in the paper): pooled output 600 sft / 2021 min = 17.8 sft per crew-hour = 1.65 m2 per crew-hour for a 1 mason + 1 helper pair; per-observation mean 1.74 m2/h, SD 0.74, CV 0.43, min 0.55, max 3.61 (n = 24). Arithmetic illustration only: x 8 h = about 13 m2 per pair-day if the undisturbed observed rate were sustained all day (it would not be; full-day losses are not measured here).
- Limitations: AAC block wall not fired brick; short observation windows; single site, one season; wall thickness, mortar and floor NR; the survey part (RII factors) is not used here. This is the only Indian measured masonry crew output with raw data that I could read in full text.
