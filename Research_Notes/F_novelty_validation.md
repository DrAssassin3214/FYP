## Executive summary (read this first)

1. Novelty: the individual mechanics are NOT new. Bernoulli-occurrence x delay Monte Carlo with mitigation and pre/post (residual) re-simulation is published at project level (F01 Prins/Kammouh 2022, F02 Kammouh 2021, F03 Hadad-Keren 2025, F04 Gheewala 2025) and at activity level for a railway station (F11 Ichsan 2025). Matrix -> MC exists (F05 Koulinas 2021, F10 Canesi 2025). EMV + MC exists (F07 Erdenekhuu 2022, F03).
2. Apparently unclaimed (moderate confidence, about 60-65% for the full a-h set; about 85% for LLM + provenance + EMV + residual re-simulation): an LLM-assisted, evidence-traced (provenance per parameter) activity-level pipeline for brick masonry (India) that maps a P-I register into Bernoulli x delay MC and ranks response alternatives by pre/post re-simulation and EMV. LLM work in construction found so far is limited to risk identification/classification (F13, F14, F15).
3. Recommended framing: claim integration + evidence traceability + masonry/India application, not the pre/post mechanism. Cite F01/F03/F04/F11 as precedents.
4. Validation: no construction paper found that reports P80 exceedance/calibration; best real back-test is F27 (302 UK projects; MC error 36.6 wk on 10 case projects). Use F33 (calibration/sharpness), F34 (V&V), F28/F30 (reference class), F61 (face-validity template), F35/F36 (expert calibration).
5. Datasets: no open masonry-activity duration dataset found. Best available: F38 (OR&S real projects, activity level, licence not stated), F66 (Zenodo work-package delays, tiny), F67 (Zenodo BoQ with masonry quantities), F37 (DoD contracts, project level).
6. Reviews: 11 open-access reviews listed (F42-F51, F65); strongest for the gaps: F45 (risk response under-studied), F65 (calls for decision-making systems), F43 (AI risk work lacks a project-management/decision view), F44 (LLM hallucination/evaluation gaps), F46 (delay-risk prediction gaps).

## 1. PART 1 - NOVELTY CHECK (most important)

### 1.1 Verdict (honest, first)

Target contribution: ONE construction activity (brick masonry, India) with (a) risk identification, (b) probability-impact matrix, (c) Bernoulli occurrence + delay-distribution Monte Carlo, (d) EMV/expected cost, (e) mitigation with residual-risk re-simulation (pre vs post), (f) decision rule comparing alternatives, (g) AI/LLM assistance, (h) explicit evidence/provenance tracing.

1. The generic method chain (c)+(e)+(f) is NOT novel. Published work already does risk-event Monte Carlo (Bernoulli occurrence x distribution) with mitigation and pre/post comparison and an optimisation/decision rule: F01 (Prins et al.), F02 (Kammouh et al. 2021), F03 (Hadad & Keren 2025, residual impact after response + expected net saving), F04 (Gheewala et al. 2025, mitigation plan cost vs days saved), and at ACTIVITY level F11 (Ichsan et al. 2025: nine critical activities, inherent vs "residual risk" after response, @RISK). Practitioner "risk driver" methods do the same in commercial tools (E3 only, snippets from projectrisk.com / Galorath pages). A claim of "first to re-simulate residual risk after a response" would be wrong and a viva examiner can find F01/F03 in minutes.
2. (a)+(b)+(c) (matrix feeding Monte Carlo, at activity level) is also already published: F05 Koulinas 2021 (risk matrix P/I per activity -> TOPSIS -> duration ranges -> MC; mitigation only as a qualitative loop), F10 Canesi et al. 2025 (probabilistic risk matrix + Bayesian MC, cost), F08 Qazi & Simsekler 2021, F19 Starczyk-Kolbyk 2025 (matrix vs MC, cost).
3. (d) EMV with MC is published: F07 Erdenekhuu 2022 (EMV = P x I, Bernoulli, MC; mining/sustainability risk, not schedule-response), F03 (expected net saving), F18 Merisalu 2021 (CBA of measures).
4. (g) LLM use in construction risk work exists but is limited to risk IDENTIFICATION/CLASSIFICATION/qualitative advice: F13 Vilibic 2026, F14 Martin-James-Chadee 2025, F15 Erfani & Khanjar 2025. I found NO paper that feeds LLM-derived parameters into a Monte Carlo + EMV pipeline (absence-of-evidence: about 10 LLM-related web searches plus 2 LLM-related Crossref queries; one search summary itself noted that no unified framework was prominent).
5. (h) explicit evidence/provenance tracing of each parameter: I found no construction-schedule-risk paper with it. Generic LLM provenance work exists outside construction (E3 only: arXiv 2606.04990 survey "From Agent Traces to Trust", MDPI "Grounded Knowledge Graph Extraction ... Provenance Tracking"). Not opened.
6. Apparently UNCLAIMED (within my search depth): the combination (g)+(h) coupled to (b)+(c)+(d)+(e)+(f) for a single repetitive activity, i.e. an LLM-assisted, literature-grounded register whose every probability/impact/mitigation-effect parameter carries a traceable evidence record, mapped from a P-I matrix into Bernoulli x delay Monte Carlo, with EMV-based comparison of response alternatives via pre/post re-simulation. Also unclaimed in what I saw: brick-masonry-specific delay-risk MC with mitigation for India (masonry/wall work appears only as "wall work was the most impactful activity" in F12, Indonesia, no mitigation).
7. Closest single competitors to cite and differentiate against: F01 (structure of Bernoulli + Beta-PERT + mitigation + pre/post probability), F03 (residual impact + expected net saving + Delphi), F04 (pre/post + mitigation cost-benefit, Indian metro rail context), F05 (matrix -> MC at activity level), F11 (activity-level pre/post residual-risk simulation, expert-panel mitigation effects). Note that "activity-level pre/post residual re-simulation" per se is therefore also NOT novel (F11).
8. Confidence: MODERATE (about 60-65% that no exact prior art covers the full (a)-(h) set; about 85% that no paper covers (g)+(h)+(e)+(d) together in construction). Reasons for not higher: about 52 WebSearch queries plus about 12 Crossref API queries and 12 Zenodo API queries only; ASCE/T&F/Elsevier full texts unread (F02, F04, F10, F14 are E2); Google Scholar/Scopus not searched; conference literature (ASCE CRC, CIB, EPOC) barely covered; the search budget ran out before I could run the last planned strings ("response-driven schedule risk analysis", "risk-adjusted project schedule with response actions" were not run as exact strings).
9. Recommended safe wording for the FYP: "an evidence-traceable, LLM-assisted, activity-level decision-support pipeline that couples a probability-impact register to Bernoulli-occurrence Monte Carlo and compares mitigation alternatives by pre/post residual-risk re-simulation and EMV" - and cite F01/F03/F04 as prior art for the pre/post mechanism rather than claiming it.

**F01. Prins, N.; Kammouh, O.; Wolfert, A.R.M. (2022). "Multi-criteria optimization and automated network restructuring to mitigate construction projects delays on-the-run - an extension of the Mitigation Controller (MitC)".** arXiv:2206.09823 (submitted 20 Jun 2022). DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2206.09823 (shown on arXiv page). Access: arXiv page states CC BY 4.0 (seen). Level E1 (full text extracted from the arXiv PDF).
- Key content (quoted): "Whether a risk occurs in a project is considered as a binary random variable X which takes the value 1 with a probability pe" ; Beta-PERT used for "activity durations, mitigating capacities, and risk durations". "The probability of completing the project within 300 days is 90% against around 30% when no mitigation strategy is applied." "the probability of completing the project within 300 days has increased from 30% to 70% by including the proposed 3 mitigation measures". Mitigation constraints in the case: investment cost <= EUR 80,000, operational <= 60,000, failure cost <= 8,000, <= 4 penalty points, <= 15,000 lost vehicle hours. Section 4.3 "Validation and practical relevance": project-organisation members acknowledged value (expert/face validation only).
- Case: Dutch DBFM tunnel construction pit, risk register (Table 3, 17 risks with probabilities).
- Limitations: network/project level, not single activity; no P-I matrix; no EMV (multi-criteria preference score 0-100); no LLM/provenance; the paper itself is an extension of F02; preprint (journal version not checked).

**F03. Hadad, Y.; Keren, B. (2025). "Risk-Response Budgeting: A Financial Optimization Approach to Project Risk Management".** J. Risk Financial Manag. 18(3), 160. DOI 10.3390/jrfm18030160. Access: OA, CC BY 4.0 (Crossref licence URL; MDPI page read in browser). Level E1 (browser, full page text).
